What this library is

A materia medica is a catalogue of medicines: what each one is made from, and the picture of symptoms it is said to answer. This one gathers the classical homeopathic materia medica — Boericke, Clarke, Kent, Allen, Nash, Farrington — into one plain-language page per remedy, and sets each portrait beside the real toxicology and modern pharmacology of its source substance. It is a study and reference aid, not medical advice.

1The idea in one paragraph

Homeopathy, founded by Samuel Hahnemann around 1800, rests on a single proposition: like cures like. A substance that produces a set of symptoms in a healthy person is given, in a very dilute and shaken form, to a patient whose illness shows a similar set of symptoms. To use it, a practitioner takes the whole case — the totality of the patient's symptoms, physical and mental — and matches it to the most similar remedy picture. Everything else in the vocabulary below follows from that.

2The words you will meet

TermIn plain words
ProvingThe old method of building a remedy's picture: healthy volunteers take a substance and record every symptom it produces. The remedy's "portrait" is the sum of those symptoms.
KeynoteA striking, characteristic symptom that strongly points to one remedy — a shortcut to the right choice.
ModalityWhat makes a symptom worse (aggravation) or better (amelioration) — time of day, temperature, motion, food, emotion.
TotalityThe whole picture of the case — mind, body, modalities, and the patient's constitution — taken together, not symptom by symptom.
ConstitutionA person's enduring make-up (build, temperament, tendencies) which some remedies are said to match — e.g. the chilly, sweaty Calcarea type.
Potency / triturationThe serial dilution-and-succussion by which a preparation is made; trituration is the grinding of the original material.
RepertoryAn index of symptoms with the remedies that have shown each one — the reverse of a materia medica.
NosodeA remedy made from diseased material (e.g. a discharge or a culture) rather than from a plant or mineral.
SarcodeA remedy made from healthy tissue or a secretion (e.g. an organ extract).
ImponderableA remedy made not from a weighable substance but from a force — sunlight, moonlight, electricity, magnetism, radiation.

3How to read a remedy page

Every portrait follows the same shape: the essence (the whole remedy in one breath), the substance (what it is made from), the traditional picture (mind, body, modalities, affinities), where the picture comes from (the real toxicology and history), the substance in real medicine (its documented modern pharmacology), look-alikes, and a safety note. Read it top to bottom and you have met the remedy whole. There is a diagram of this on the About page.

4About the science sections

Each remedy page has two halves that are deliberately kept apart. The traditional picture reports what the classical authors recorded. The substance in real medicine reports what the source material actually does in modern pharmacology — the arsenic that treats leukaemia, the foxglove that is digoxin, the bee venom that cures sting allergy. Where the source substance has no modern medical use, the page says so plainly. The research debate on ultra-dilute preparations themselves — the supporting work and the placebo consensus — is set out once, on the Evidence page.

A note on reading critically. This library presents the classical literature faithfully and the modern science honestly, side by side, and lets you weigh them. A vivid remedy portrait is not proof that a remedy works; it is a description of a tradition. Nothing here is medical advice, and nothing here should delay proper care.
