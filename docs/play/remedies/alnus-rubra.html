Alnus rubra

Made from the red alder bark — the Pacific tree of the Eclectic dispensaries, a genuine astringent of the old pharmacy, and the classical remedy of the chronic skin and the swollen glands.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a skin and glands that never settle: the chronic eczema and eruptions of the books, the swollen lymphatics, the female complaints of the Eclectic chapter, and the red-barked alder of the Pacific forests. The red-alder remedy: that is Alnus rubra.

The substance, in plain words

The red alder is the fast-growing tree of the Pacific coast, its red bark a genuine astringent of the Eclectic dispensaries — its tannins really contract and dry, used for the chronic skin and the glands. The proving gave the alder the chronic-skin portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about appearance. Worry over the skin, the glands, the chronicity, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the chronic discomfort.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the long, recurrent states.

The body

  • Chronic eczema and eruptions. The keynote: the chronic, weeping, recurrent skin of the books' Eclectic chapter.
  • Swollen lymphatics. The swollen glands and lymphatics of the same chapter.
  • Female complaints. The books add the female complaints of the same shelf.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night thicken the skin and the glands.
  • Better for the bark's course and dry weather. The astringent course and the dry season ease the alder state.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Worse from ⬇Better from ⬇
cold damp weather; night; rich food.the bark's course; dry weather; rest.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Skin (chronic eczema), glands and lymphatics, female organs, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic bark's proving gave the chronic-skin portrait; Alnus rubra became the classical remedy of the skin and glands that never settle — the red alder of the Pacific serving the chronicity its tannins dried.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

The red alder's bark is genuinely astringent — its tannins the real chemistry of the Eclectic skin chapter, drying and contracting as tannins do. The chronic eczema it portrayed is now dermatology's, with real, effective treatments; the bark's chemistry is honest, its portrait's territory modern.
