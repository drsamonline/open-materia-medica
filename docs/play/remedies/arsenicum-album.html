Arsenicum album

Made from arsenic trioxide (As₂O₃) — a white powder that is a deadly poison in overdose, and a genuine leukaemia drug in controlled hospital doses.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a person who is frightened and cannot keep still, who feels burning pains yet is shivering and craves warmth, who drinks tiny sips, again and again, and whose stomach and bowels are in uproar — often after spoiled food or drink. The hours around midnight are the worst. They are chilly, pale, maybe sweating a cold sweat, exhausted out of all proportion — yet anxious, especially afraid of death, and desperate for company and reassurance. That person is the classical portrait of Arsenicum album.

The substance, in plain words

Arsenic is a grey metal famous for centuries as a poison — so notorious it was once called "inheritance powder." Its white oxide, arsenic trioxide, is what homeopathic pharmacies grind and dilute to make this remedy. It is also, remarkably, a modern medicine: given by intravenous drip in carefully controlled doses (as the drug Trisenox), arsenic trioxide is an approved treatment for acute promyelocytic leukaemia, where it destroys leukaemic cells by targeting their PML-RARα fusion protein [10][11][12]. It works because it is poisonous — which is exactly where the classical picture comes from (below).

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety that moves. The hallmark is restlessness with fear — pacing, tossing, changing place, yet the anxiety does not lift. Kent built his lectures around this anxious agitation.
  • Fear of death, dread of being alone. They want company and constant reassurance — "say I will get better" — and may fear robbers, illness, poverty, or disaster.
  • Midnight anguish. Worst after midnight, roughly 12–2 a.m.: restlessness, fear, asthma, and diarrhoea all flare in those hours.
  • Fastidious, driven temperament. In health, the "Arsenicum type" is tidy, order-loving, anxious about details and money.

The body

  • Burning, yet chilly. Burning pains (throat, stomach, abdomen, skin) that paradoxically feel better from heat — hot drinks, warm wraps, sitting by the fire. This "burning relieved by heat" is one of the most famous keynotes in the whole library.
  • Thirst for small sips. Sips rather than gulps — the opposite of the Bryonia type, who gulps large amounts.
  • Gut crisis after bad food. Violent vomiting and diarrhoea resembling food poisoning, with burning pain, prostration, and often a cold sweat on the face or forehead. Clarke used it for food-poisoning states; Nash for cholera-like collapse.
  • Prostration out of proportion. Profound weakness, sinking strength — Allen's keynote: wherever Arsenicum fits, the vital forces seem to drop rapidly.
  • Skin. Dry, rough, scaly, intensely itchy or burning; scratching burns; worse from cold water.
  • Breathing. Asthma that worsens after midnight, forcing them to sit up and lean forward.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

The digestive tube (stomach and bowel), the skin, the airways (asthmatic states), and the nervous system — hence its old nickname as a "food-poisoning" and "collapse" remedy.

Where the classical picture comes from

The portrait of Arsenicum album is, essentially, a clinical description of acute arsenic poisoning: violent gastroenteritis, burning abdominal pain, thirst, restlessness, cold clammy sweat, and collapse are the textbook signs of arsenic toxicity [13]. Nineteenth-century authors recorded these effects from poisonings and provings and matched them to similar states of illness — the reason this remedy's picture is so vivid.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

In full, measurable doses, arsenic trioxide is one of oncology's genuine success stories: combined with ATRA (all-trans retinoic acid) it is front-line treatment for acute promyelocytic leukaemia, curing most low-risk patients — about 97% event-free survival at two years in the pivotal trial [11][12]. It achieves this by degrading the PML-RARα fusion protein that drives the leukaemia and pushing the cancerous cells to mature and die [10]. Same molecule, completely different dose and context.

Safety. Severe or persistent vomiting and diarrhoea, suspected poisoning, breathing difficulty, or collapse are emergencies — call medical services.
