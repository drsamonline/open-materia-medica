Avena sativa

Made from the oat plant — the grain of the breakfast table whose green-tops are a genuine modern nervine tonic, and the classical remedy of nervous exhaustion.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a nervous system spent past its reserves: trembling weakness after illness or overwork, sleeplessness with the nerves too tired to rest, the inability to concentrate, and the books' great restorative of the exhausted nerve. The spent-nerves restorative: that is Avena sativa.

The substance, in plain words

The oat is the grain of the world's breakfast tables, its green tops gathered before the grain ripens and used as a genuine nervine tonic — the Eclectic school's great restorative, and a modern herbal standard for exhaustion and sleeplessness with real, if modest, research behind it. The proving gave the remedy the spent-nerves portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Inability to concentrate. The keynote of the mind: the spent nerves cannot hold a thought — work becomes impossible.
  • Anxiety and dread. A generalised dread and worry, worse in the quiet hours.
  • Irritable exhaustion. Cross from weakness, not from anger — the books' portrait of the tired nerve.

The body

  • Trembling weakness after illness. The keynote: the drained, trembling weakness after fevers, overwork or grief — the great convalescent of the books.
  • Sleeplessness of the exhausted. Too tired to sleep — the body worn and the nerves still humming.
  • Numbness and aching. The books record the numb, aching limbs of the debilitated state.
  • Palpitations of the exhaustion. The heart flutters with the least effort of the spent system.
  • Worse exertion and the quiet hours. Effort drains further; the quiet hours expose the emptiness.
  • Better for rest and nourishment. Rest, nourishment and time restore what the spent nerves lost.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic restoratives proving gave the spent-nerves portrait; Avena became the classical remedy of nervous exhaustion — the green oat tops serving the nerves the breakfast table serves the body.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Oat tops are genuinely among the best-regarded herbal nervines: used in the Eclectic and modern traditions for exhaustion and sleeplessness, with laboratory evidence for calming and restorative effects — modest but real. The breakfast grain's green twin is one of herbal medicine's most respectable restoratives, and a rare case where folk use and modern pharmacology agree in tone if not in strength.
