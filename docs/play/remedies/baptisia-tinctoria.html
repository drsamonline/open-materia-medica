Baptisia tinctoria

Made from wild indigo — the American prairie herb of the Eclectic fever wards, a genuine antiseptic, and the classical remedy of the dark, drowsy typhoid state.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a fever that turns the face dark: drowsiness sinking toward stupor, a dusky, dark-red, almost sooty face, the body aching as if bruised, the bed hard, and the strange delirium of the books — the sufferer imagines his own limbs scattered about the bed, and reaches for them. The dark drowsy typhoid state: that is Baptisia.

The substance, in plain words

Wild indigo is the prairie herb of the American midwest, its roots a genuine antiseptic of the Eclectic fever wards — used in typhoid and the septic fevers before antibiotics, with documented antibacterial chemistry. The proving gave the remedy its famous dark, drowsy, disordered portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Delirium of scattered limbs. The celebrated keynote: the sufferer imagines the body in pieces — reaches for the scattered limbs, talks to them — the books' most quoted typhoid delirium.
  • Drowsiness to stupor. The heavy sinking sleepiness of the low fevers.
  • Anxiety and fear. Fear of death, of poison, worse at night — under the drowsiness.

The body

  • Dark dusky face. The keynote: the dusky, dark-red, sooty discoloration of the fevered face.
  • Bruised, beaten aching. The body aches as if pounded — the books' typhoid soreness.
  • The bed feels hard. Like Arnica's — no position comfortable in the low fever.
  • Worse night and motion. The drowsiness and the aching deepen at night; motion aggravates.
  • Typhoid and septic fevers. The books' portrait of the low, lingering, offensive fevers of the pre-antibiotic wards.
  • Better for rest and quiet Stillness and quiet ease the bruised, drowsy state.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic fever-ward drug — genuine antiseptic, genuinely used in typhoid — gave the proving its frame; the dark dusky face, the scattered-limbs delirium and the hard bed became the remedy's identity, read in every old typhoid chapter.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Baptisia was a genuine, documented antiseptic of the Eclectic school, used in typhoid fever with real antibacterial chemistry behind it. The disease it portrayed — typhoid — is now a near-vanquished infection in the developed world: antibiotics cure it, vaccines and clean water prevent it, and the dark drowsy wards of the old books are medical history.

Safety. High fever with confusion, drowsiness or a dark face is an emergency — sepsis and meningitis are assessed, not watched.
