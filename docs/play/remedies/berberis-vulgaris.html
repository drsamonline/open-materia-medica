Berberis vulgaris

Made from common barberry — the hedge shrub whose berberine is one of herbal pharmacology's best-studied alkaloids, and the classical kidney-stone remedy.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of pains that bubble and radiate: a bubbling, stitching sensation in the kidney region shooting down to the bladder, backache with a bruised, beaten feeling in the small of the back, gravel in the urine, and joints that feel swollen without looking it. Radiating bubbling kidney pains: that is Berberis.

The substance, in plain words

Barberry is the spiny hedge shrub with red berries and yellow wood — the yellow announcing berberine, an isoquinoline alkaloid that stains everything gold. Berberine is among the most-studied plant alkaloids alive, and the remedy is the classical portrait of renal colic and radiating backache.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Discouragement with pain. Low spirits, anxiety about health, weariness in company during the long renal states.
  • Irritability. Crossness from the dragging, never-quite-still pains.
  • Despondency in chronic cases. The books note the hopeless mood of the gravel sufferer.

The body

  • Radiating kidney pains. The keynote: stitching, bubbling pains from the kidney region radiating to bladder, urethra, thighs — changing direction constantly.
  • Bruised backache. Lumbago with a beaten, bruised small-of-back feeling, worse standing or sitting still
  • Gravel and sediment. Urine with gravel, red sediment, jelly-like mucus; burning at the close of urination.
  • Swelling without inflammation. Joints puffy and tender without redness — the books' 'phantom swelling.'
  • Wandering twinges. Pains that dart about — hepatic, renal, articular — never staying put.
  • Liver stitches. Right hypochondrium stitches radiating to the back; the remedy of the whole right-sided plumbing.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Kidneys and urinary tract, liver and gall region, back and joints.

Where the classical picture comes from

The berberine-rich root proved with twinging, radiating pains and urinary sediment; the remedy then became the classical companion of the stone-and-gravel sufferer, whose colics filled the surgical casebooks of the era.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Berberine is genuinely one of pharmacology's most-investigated plant alkaloids: studies explore its effects on blood sugar, cholesterol and gut infection, and it is a standard traditional medicine in several systems. Its clinical strength in modern medicine remains under confirmation, but its chemistry is unquestionably active — barberry is no placebo-plant.

Safety. Colicky pain with fever, blood in urine, or inability to pass urine needs urgent assessment — a stuck stone is an emergency.
