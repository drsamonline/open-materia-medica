Calcarea fluorica

Made from calcium fluoride — the mineral of enamel and bone, and the classical remedy of hard, stony, elastic-tissue states.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of tissues losing their elasticity and turning stony: hardened glands, varicose and knotted veins, loose teeth, stretching and cracking of the skin, bone-spurs, and a general constitutional chilliness. The hardening, the loosening teeth, the knotted veins: that is Calcarea fluorica.

The substance, in plain words

Calcium fluoride is the mineral of tooth enamel and bone surfaces — the hardest chemistry in the body. The biochemic school made it the remedy of 'elastic tissue gone wrong': whatever stretches too far or hardens too much, from varicose veins to bone-spurs.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about health. Fear of the hardening, of the growths; low spirits in the chronic states.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction.
  • Despondency. Gloomy foreboding of degeneration.

The body

  • Hardened glands and stony growths. The keynote: induration — glands, tonsils, breasts, testes that turn hard and stony.
  • Varicose, knotted veins. Veins that knot and dilate; the books pair it with the haemorrhoidal and varicose constitution.
  • Loosening teeth. Teeth that loosen in their sockets — the fluoride-affinity showing in the portrait.
  • Cracking, hard skin. Skin that fissures, thickens, loses its stretch.
  • Bone-spurs and exostoses. Bony outgrowths, heel-spurs, the hard projections of the books.
  • Worse damp and change of weather. The hard parts ache in wet and with the weather's turn.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Elastic tissues (veins, skin, ligaments), bones and teeth, glands (hardened), connective tissue.

Where the classical picture comes from

The biochemic school framed Calc-fluor as the salt of elastic tissue; the proving gave the hard-and-stony portrait, and the remedy attached to every induration the era's surgeons palpated — from tonsils to varicose knots.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Fluoride's real medical story is enormous and double-edged: in water and toothpaste it genuinely prevents dental caries — one of preventive medicine's cheapest triumphs — while excess causes fluorosis, mottled enamel and skeletal hardening. The mineral that hardens enamel is the same one that, in excess, hardens bone: the remedy's portrait walks the same line.
