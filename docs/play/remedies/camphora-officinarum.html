Camphora

Made from camphor — the white crystalline essence of the camphor tree, first aid of the East for centuries and a collapse remedy in the classics.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a person turning to ice: generalised icy coldness — cold breath, cold sweat, blue lips — that astonishingly is not relieved by blankets or hot-water bottles. Collapse after exposure, vomiting with coldness, anxiety with the chill. Icy coldness unrelieved by warmth: that is Camphora.

The substance, in plain words

Camphor is the crystalline essence distilled from the camphor laurel of Asia. It is a genuine drug — rubefacient, decongestant, antipruritic — and a genuine poison: swallowed doses cause convulsions, while the classics knew it as the great reviver of the collapsed. Every household of the East kept camphor for faintness.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the coldness. Fear, starting, desire not to be left alone in the sinking state.
  • Delirium and excitement. The proving produced furious excitement and delirium alternating with collapse — camphor's two faces.
  • Irritability at being covered. Paradoxically worse for the warmth of coverings despite the coldness.

The body

  • Icy coldness not relieved by warmth. The keynote: whole-body coldness, cold breath, cold sweat, that blankets and hot bottles fail to relieve.
  • Collapse states. Fainting, shock after exposure or injury; the first-stage 'cold stage' remedy of the old acute triad (Camphora, Veratrum, Carbo veg).
  • One-sided coldness. One hand or one side cold — a curious confirmatory sign in the books.
  • Vomiting with chill. Vomiting of what was just drunk, with the general coldness.
  • Cramps and convulsions. Muscular jerks, convulsive movements in the poisoned picture.
  • Skin and eruptions. The books use it when a rash or sweat is suppressed by cold, and the patient turns cold and anxious.

Where the classical picture comes from

Camphor poisoning and camphor first-aid were equally well known: the poison causes excitement then collapse with coldness; the household crystal revives the faint. The proving wove both into the remedy's portrait of coldness that will not warm.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Camphor is a real, registered drug: vapour rubs and camphorated oils for congestion, chest rubs, antipruritic lotions — and it remains a classic toxicology case for seizures in children who swallow the crystals. Its dual identity, stimulant rub and convulsant poison, is precisely the tension the classical picture captures.

Safety. Camphor is dangerous if swallowed — seizures can follow even small ingestions; keep crystals away from children and seek emergency help for any ingestion.
