Cascara sagrada

Made from cascara bark — the American buckthorn whose anthraquinone laxative remained in the pharmacopoeias into living memory, and the classical remedy of the obstinate, portal-bound constipation.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a bowel bound to the portal system: obstinate, chronic constipation with a sensation of fullness in the portal region, the old books' association with jaundice and the torpid liver, and the remedy of the dried-up, bound constitution. The portal-bound constipation: that is Cascara.

The substance, in plain words

Cascara — the bark of the American buckthorn — was a genuine, official laxative of the pharmacopoeias for over a century, its anthraquinone chemistry working the bowel the same way as its cousins senna and the yellow dock of the library. It is among the last of the botanical purgatives to retire; the proving gave the remedy its portal-constipation portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability and gloom. Cross, low-spirited, worse in the morning with the bound state.
  • Anxiety about health. Worry over the chronicity, the habit.
  • Dullness. Heavy mind with the portal congestion.

The body

  • Obstinate chronic constipation. The keynote: the bound, habitual bowel of the books' chronic chapter — no urging, no result.
  • Portal fullness. A sensation of fullness and congestion in the portal region — the liver-and-rectum pairing.
  • Jaundice association. The old books' link to the sluggish, jaundiced liver.
  • Worse cold damp and morning. Wet weather and the waking hours aggravate the bound state.
  • Sedentary link. The books pair it with the sedentary, portal-congested life.
  • Better for the bowel's movement and warmth. Warmth, motion and the reluctant evacuation bring the only relief.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Bowels (chronic constipation), liver and portal system, rectum, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The official laxative's century-long career gave the proving its frame — the bound bowel and the congested portal; Cascara became the classical remedy of the habitual, portal-bound constipation, standing at the respectable end of the purge family.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Cascara was a genuine, official laxative of the pharmacopoeias — one of the last botanical purgatives in mainstream use, retired as modern bowel medicine advanced. Its anthraquinone chemistry really works — and really warns: chronic stimulant-laxative use is now managed carefully for dependence and electrolyte loss. The bark's career spans the whole era of the purge, honourably concluded.
