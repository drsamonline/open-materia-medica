Coccus cacti

Made from cochineal — the cactus-dwelling insect that dyes the world red as carmine, and the classical cough remedy with the thread of pink mucus.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a whooping cough with a signature: violent paroxysms ending in the expectoration of bright red mucus drawn out in fine threads — the cochineal's own dye showing in the sputum — worse at night, in warm rooms, on waking. Thread-like pink mucus: that is Coccus cacti.

The substance, in plain words

The cochineal insect lives on cacti and dyes history: carmine — the red of lipstick, food colouring and art — is its body chemistry, harvested for centuries. The proving gave the classical whooping-cough portrait whose expectoration arrives in fine red threads.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the paroxysms. Fear, tears and suffocation during the fits; worry in the warm night hours.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse for contradiction during the illness.
  • Low spirits. Gloom accompanies the long convalescence of the whooping cough era.

The body

  • Thread-like mucus threads. The keynote: fine, bright, thread-like streaks of mucus, sometimes blood-tinged, raised after the paroxysm.
  • Whooping paroxysms. Violent, choking fits, worse night and on first lying.
  • Worse warm rooms. Heat and close rooms aggravate; open air relieves.
  • Worse on waking. The fits greet the morning with full force.
  • Hemorrhage with coughing. Nosebleed or blood-flecked sputum from the violence of the fits.
  • Kidney shadow. The old books note a renal association — gravel and sediment in the same patients.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Respiratory tract (paroxysmal cough), urinary tract (gravel), mucous membranes.

Where the classical picture comes from

The insect's dye chemistry coloured the proving's signature symptom — the red, thread-like expectoration; the remedy served the pertussis wards of the era beside Drosera, Corallium and Cuprum.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Cochineal's carmine is genuinely industrial: a stable natural red still used in food (E120), cosmetics and art when synthetic reds are avoided. The insect's chemistry is real and traded globally — one of the library's most commercially important sources, and entirely non-medicinal in its modern life.

Safety. Paroxysmal cough in a child with vomiting, breath-holding or bleeding needs medical assessment; pertussis is preventable by vaccination.
