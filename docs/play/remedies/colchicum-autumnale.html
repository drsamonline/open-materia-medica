Colchicum autumnale

Made from the autumn crocus — the meadow saffron whose colchicine remains a genuine gout drug and whose chemistry teaches cell division itself, and the classical remedy of the swollen, agonising joint.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of gout in its purest drama: a joint — the great toe, the knee — swollen beyond recognition, red, shiny, agonising at the slightest touch, so that even the bedsheet's weight is torture, with nausea and faintness riding the attack, and an odd sensitivity to smells at its height. The touch-me-not swollen joint: that is Colchicum.

The substance, in plain words

The autumn crocus is the meadow saffron of European autumns. Its colchicine is a genuine, ancient gout drug — still in use today — and a genuine mitotic poison, arresting cell division, used in laboratories to teach genetics and in research as a chromosome-doubling agent. Few plants carry so much real pharmacology in one alkaloid.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability and despair. Cross, wretched, worse during the attack; the books record the gouty temperament faithfully.
  • Anxiety and fear. Fear of the next attack, dread of the touch.
  • Depression in autumn The attacks and the mood arrive with the season.

The body

  • Swollen, agonising, untouchable joints. The keynote: joints so swollen and tender that the lightest touch — a sheet, a breath of air — is unbearable.
  • The great toe and the knee. The classical gout sites — the books describe them in the detail only a sufferer could supply.
  • Nausea and faintness with the attack. The systemic sickness of the severe attack — vomiting, sinking, prostration.
  • Smell-sensitivity at the height. The quoted peculiarity — the sense of smell becomes unbearably acute with the worst of the pain.
  • Worse motion and touch. The joint cannot bear touch, motion, or the least jarring.
  • Better for rest and quiet Perfect stillness of the part and quiet ease the agony.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

The gout of the rich table and the wine cellar gave the proving its drama — the untouchable joint, the autumn return, the smell that cannot be borne. Colchicum became the classical gout remedy, standing beside its own alkaloid's real career in the dispensary.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Colchicine is one of medicine's oldest working drugs: used for gout for two millennia, still prescribed today, its mechanism (microtubule inhibition) understood to the molecule. The same chemistry arrests cell division — laboratories use it to double chromosomes, and its narrow safety margin keeps toxicology vigilant. A genuine drug, a genuine poison, one molecule.
