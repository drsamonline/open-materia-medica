Collinsonia canadensis

Made from stone root — the hard, knotted American herb of old vein remedies, and the classical companion of piles and pelvic congestion.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a pelvis congested: hemorrhoids that burn and bleed with constipation alternating with looseness, a raw, dry sensation in the rectum, a sensation of sticks and prickling in the pelvic floor, and the odd alternation of piles and heart symptoms. Pelvic congestion, piles with heart, prickling floor: that is Collinsonia.

The substance, in plain words

Stone root is the hard-knotted American woodland herb whose knotted rhizome resembles the haemorrhoids it was used for — the old 'signature' logic. Its chemistry is genuinely astringent and venotonic; the Eclectics used it as a real remedy for piles and pelvic congestion.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Depression of the chronic sufferer. Low spirits, irritability, worse with the rectal and pelvic pains.
  • Anxiety about health. The books pair it with the worried pile sufferer of sedentary life.
  • Lassitude. General tiredness with the congested pelvis.

The body

  • Hemorrhoids with alternating bowels. The keynote: piles with constipation alternating with diarrhea; the rectum alternates between bound and loose.
  • Burning, bleeding piles. Piles that burn and smart and ooze, worse walking and worse sitting.
  • Prickling pelvic floor. A sensation of sticks, prickling and constriction in the rectum and pelvic floor.
  • Piles and heart paired. The old books' strange double: rectal congestion with palpitation and lancinating chest pains.
  • Raw, dry rectum. A sensation of dryness and rawness, as of a stiff hair, in the passage.
  • Worse morning and after stool. The pelvic aching is worst on waking and after evacuation.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Rectum and pelvic veins, circulation (heart), bowel, pelvic organs.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic vein-remedy tradition gave Collinsonia its frame — knotted root for knotted veins; the proving confirmed the pelvic-congestion portrait, and the remedy entered the classical library as the piles-with-heart companion.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Stone root's astringent-venotonic chemistry made it a genuine Eclectic drug of the nineteenth century, retired but real; modern haemorrhoid care runs from fibre and fluids to rubber-band ligation and surgery, with the pelvic congestion the books described now managed as varicose and pelvic-floor disease.
