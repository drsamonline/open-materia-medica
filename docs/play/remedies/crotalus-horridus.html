Crotalus horridus

Made from rattlesnake venom — the hemotoxin of the American diamondback, and the classical remedy of dark blood and sinking fevers.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of dark, oozing states: blood that is black and fluid and will not clot, purplish-blotched skin, drowsiness deepening toward stupor, yellowing of the eyes, and the peculiar worse-lying-on-the-left-side — the snakebite picture internalised. Dark bleeding, drowsy sinking, left-side worse: that is Crotalus.

The substance, in plain words

Rattlesnake venom is a brew of enzymes that digest tissue, burst blood vessels and block clotting — the hemotoxic snakebite that swells, bruises and bleeds. Antivenom is one of modern medicine's genuine triumphs; the venom itself remains a goldmine of research compounds.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Drowsiness to stupor. The mental keynote: sleepy, heavy, half-conscious sinking, answering slowly — Lachesis talks, Crotalus sleeps.
  • Anxiety and fear. Fear of death, fear of being poisoned, low muttering delirium in the febrile states.
  • Forgetfulness. Absent-minded, loses the thread, forgets words — the books note a 'thick' mind in the venom state.

The body

  • Dark, unclotted bleeding. The keynote: oozing black blood from any orifice or wound; haemorrhages where the blood refuses to clot.
  • Purple-blotched skin. Ecchymosis, mottled purple patches, swelling with discoloration — the snakebite's bruise without the bite.
  • Jaundice and dusky yellow. Sinking fevers with yellow eyes and dark skin — the old books' 'yellow fever' companion.
  • Worse lying on the left side. A strongly quoted modality: the whole state worsens lying left; must turn.
  • Eye signs. Bleeding into the whites of the eyes, drooping lids, visual dimming.
  • Low septic states. The books apply it to the drowsy, foul, sinking states of severe infection, where the patient sleeps and darkens.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Blood and clotting, skin and tissues, heart and circulation, liver (jaundiced states).

Where the classical picture comes from

Rattlesnake envenomation was among the best-described poisonings in the Americas: swelling, bruising, bleeding gums, drowsiness and collapse. The proving of the venom restated the toxicology, and the remedy became the classical portrait of dark-blooded, sinking sepsis.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Snakebite medicine is a modern success story: antivenom, made by immunising horses with venom, saves tens of thousands of lives yearly, and venom compounds are mined for new drugs — from blood-pressure agents to anticancer research. Rattlesnake venom's clot-busting enzymes are studied for stroke treatment. Few sources join folk danger and modern pharmacology so directly.
