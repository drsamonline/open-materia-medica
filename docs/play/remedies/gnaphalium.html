Gnaphalium polycephalum

Made from cudweed — the woolly everlasting of American meadows, and the classical remedy of the sciatica with numbness.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a sciatic nerve that burns then numbs: intense sciatic pain down the back of the thigh and leg, worse lying and sitting, relieved by flexing the leg on the abdomen or walking bent; and between the pains, a creeping numbness of the part. The sciatica-with-numbness remedy: that is Gnaphalium.

The substance, in plain words

Cudweed is the woolly, everlasting-flowered herb of American meadows, its leaves once used in the smoking mixtures of the Indigenous peoples. Its chemistry is modest but real — the cudweed's essential oils are genuinely active. The proving gave the remedy its sciatic portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the pain. Worry and dread of the next paroxysm, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant, exhausting nerve pain.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, recurrent states.

The body

  • Sciatica with numbness. The keynote: intense sciatic pain running down the posterior thigh and leg, with creeping numbness of the part — the books' celebrated combination.
  • Worse lying and sitting. Recumbency and sitting drive the pain wild; the sufferer must move.
  • Better for flexing the thigh. Drawing the leg up on the abdomen, or walking bent, relieves — the quoted amelioration.
  • Chronic rheumatic pains. The books extend it to the chronic rheumatic pains of the damp season.
  • Worse damp cold and night. Wet weather and the night bring the lightning down the nerve.
  • Better for gentle motion. Moving gently about eases the nerve; rest and pressure aggravate.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Sciatic nerve and lower limbs, general nerves, rheumatic tissue.

Where the classical picture comes from

The cudweed's proving gave the sciatica-with-numbness portrait — one of the library's most precise nerve pictures; Gnaphalium became the classical remedy of the posterior sciatic pain that only flexion relieves.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Cudweed's essential-oil chemistry is genuinely active — studied for anti-inflammatory effects in laboratory models — though no drug emerged. The sciatica it portrayed is now neurology's and pain medicine's: examined, imaged and treated with real, effective modern pharmacology when it persists.
