Hydrocotyle

Made from gotu kola — the pennywort of Asian medicine, its asiaticoside a genuine wound-healing compound of modern pharmacology, and the classical remedy of the thickened skin.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of skin that thickens and will not soften: psoriasis-like thickening, the lupus-like states of the old books, the chronic ulcers and the thickened scars, and the little creeping herb of the Asian marshes — a genuine modern wound medicine. The thickening-skin remedy: that is Hydrocotyle.

The substance, in plain words

Gotu kola is the creeping pennywort of Asian marshes, eaten as a salad and revered as a longevity herb — and a genuine subject of modern pharmacology: its asiaticoside is studied and used for wound healing and skin repair, a real, active wound-healing compound. The proving gave the herb the thickened-skin portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about appearance. Worry over the thickening skin, the scars, the chronicity, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the chronic discomfort.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the long, recurrent states.

The body

  • Thickened skin states. The keynote: the thickened, hardened skin — the psoriasis-like states and the thickened scars of the books.
  • Lupus-like states. The books' great use — the lupus-like, ulcerating skin of the old chapter.
  • Chronic ulcers. The slow, weeping ulcers the herb's wound chemistry serves.
  • Burning and itching. The burning, itching thickened patches of the proving.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night thicken the skin further.
  • Better for the herb's course and rest. The slow softening course and rest ease the thickened state.

Where the classical picture comes from

The marsh pennywort's proving gave the thickened-skin portrait; Hydrocotyle became the classical remedy of the thickening skin and the chronic ulcer — the Asian wound herb serving the scars its chemistry heals.
