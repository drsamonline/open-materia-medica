Lachesis muta

Made from the venom of the bushmaster, South America's giant viper — whose peptides taught modern medicine how to lower blood pressure.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a hot, talkative, passionate person — purple-flushed, worse on the left side, who cannot bear anything touching the throat or waist, cannot bear tight collars, and wakes worse than they went to sleep. Words pour out, jealousy simmers, and everything left-sided swells, darkens and throbs. Purple, loquacious, left-sided and touch-intolerant: that is Lachesis.

The substance, in plain words

The bushmaster is the largest viper in the Americas, and its venom is a pharmacy of peptides and enzymes: it drops blood pressure, dissolves tissue, prevents clotting and floods bites with swelling and bruising. The homeopathic preparation is made from the milked venom — and the venom family produced captopril, the first ACE-inhibitor blood-pressure drug.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Overflowing talkativeness. Speech rushes in torrents, jumping subjects; cannot bear interruption — and jumps from one idea to the next.
  • Passion and jealousy. Intense feelings, suspicion, jealousy and wounded pride; vivid dream life of pursuit and danger.
  • Worse after sleep. The famous 'sleeps into' aggravation — they wake worse than on lying down, and cannot bear to sleep on the affected side.

The body

  • Purple and blue discoloration. Bruised, bluish-purple swellings; the books use it where tissue looks dark and congested.
  • Left-sidedness. Complaints begin on the left or move right; left ovary, left tonsil, left breast.
  • Throat intolerance. Sore throat worse from empty swallowing, better for swallowing solids — and the slightest touch of clothing at the neck or waist cannot be borne.
  • Hot flushes. Surges of heat with palpitation, especially at the menopause, relieved when sweat or a discharge begins.
  • Bleeding tendencies. Dark, oozing, non-clotting blood; the old books applied it to snakebite-like states.
  • Sensitivity to touch and pressure. Pain at the slightest touch, intolerance of tight clothes, belts and collars.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Blood and circulation, throat and neck, female organs (especially at menopause), skin discoloration.

Where the classical picture comes from

Bushmaster envenomation produces exactly the remedy's portrait: rapid swelling, purple bruising, bleeding, surging pulse, prostration and sometimes talkative agitation. Snake-venom poisoning was well described by nineteenth-century travellers and toxicologists, giving the classical authors their raw material.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

The bushmaster's relatives in the pit-viper family gave medicine one of its great drugs: bradykinin-potentiating peptides from Bothrops venom led directly to captopril, the first ACE inhibitor (1975), which transformed high-blood-pressure treatment. Venom peptides remain an active frontier of drug discovery for pain, clotting and blood pressure — the family's real pharmacology is undeniable.
