Lupulinum

Made from the hop's lupulin — the yellow powder of the brewer's flower, a genuine sedative of real modern evidence, and the classical remedy of the nervous sleeplessness.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of nerves too twitchy for sleep: nervous irritability with sleeplessness, the genital oversensitivity of the proving, the restlessness of the books' hop chapter, and the yellow powder the brewer shakes from his flowers. The brewer's-sedative remedy: that is Lupulinum.

The substance, in plain words

Lupulin is the yellow powder dusted from the brewer's hop flowers — the bitter resins that flavour the beer and genuinely calm the nervous system: humulone and lupulone are studied sedatives with real, if modest, modern evidence, and the hop pillow is a folk sleep-aid of long standing. The proving gave the powder the sleepless-irritability portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Nervous irritability before sleep. The keynote of the mind: the twitchy, irritable over-wakefulness — the nervous system's refusal to dismount.
  • Anxiety and fear. Worry and dread, worse in the quiet hours.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, sleepless states.

The body

  • Sleeplessness of nervousness. The keynote: the sleeplessness of the over-wrought nerve — the books' hop use.
  • Genital oversensitivity. The proving's genital chapter — the oversensitivity and excitement of the books.
  • Restlessness and twitching. The books add the restless, twitching limbs of the nervous state.
  • Headache of nervousness. The congestive headache riding the sleeplessness.
  • Worse the quiet and the evening. Solitude and evening bring the state on.
  • Better for open air and company. Fresh air and gentle company ease the twitchy nerve.

Where the classical picture comes from

The brewer's powder's proving gave the sleepless-irritability portrait; Lupulinum became the classical remedy of the twitchy, sleepless nerve — the hop's yellow dust serving the nervous system it brewers have long used it to calm.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

The hop's resins are genuinely studied: humulone and lupulone have documented sedative and calming effects in laboratory research, and the hop pillow's folk use for sleep has real chemistry behind it — modest but honest. The brewer's flower is one of the few folk sedatives modern pharmacology takes seriously at all.
