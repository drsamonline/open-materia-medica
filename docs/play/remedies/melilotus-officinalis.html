Melilotus officinalis

Made from sweet clover — the meadow herb whose coumarin chemistry gave the world the anticoagulant family, and the classical remedy of the congestive headache relieved by a nosebleed.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a head congested beyond bearing: throbbing, bursting, congestive headaches with a dazed, stupid feeling, relieved in an instant by a nosebleed — the books' celebrated amelioration — and the sensibility of the ears roaring with the pressure. The relieved-by-bleeding headache: that is Melilotus.

The substance, in plain words

Sweet clover is the tall meadow herb of Europe and Asia, its leaves carrying coumarin — the fragrant molecule whose spoiled-hay chemistry became dicoumarol and then warfarin, the anticoagulant family that changed medicine. The proving gave the remedy its congestive portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Dazed and stupid with the congestion. The keynote of the mind: a dazed, heavy, half-stupid feeling with the bursting headache.
  • Anxiety and fear. Worry about the headaches, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise, which also pounds in the ears.

The body

  • Congestive headache relieved by nosebleed. The keynote: violent throbbing congestion of the head, relieved the moment the nose bleeds — the quoted amelioration.
  • Roaring in the ears. The pressure fills the ears with roaring and sensitivity to sound.
  • Flushed face with the pain. A congested, hot face with the bursting headache.
  • Worse rainy weather and night. Wet weather and the night deepen the congestion.
  • Vomiting with the headache. The books add the sickness at the height of the pain.
  • Better for bleeding and open air. The nosebleed relieves; cool open air eases the pressure.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Head (congestion), ears, circulation, eyes.

Where the classical picture comes from

The meadow clover's proving gave the relieved-by-bleeding portrait; the old books applied it to the congestive headaches of the plethoric — and the plant's own chemistry would, decades later, give medicine the bleeding family of drugs.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Coumarin is genuinely one of chemistry's most consequential molecules: the spoiled-sweet-clover hay that made cattle bleed led to dicoumarol and warfarin — the anticoagulants that prevent strokes and treat clots, among the most-prescribed drugs in the world. The plant whose headache is relieved by a nosebleed gave medicine the chemistry of controlled bleeding: the symmetry is perfect.

Safety. Severe or new headaches, or headaches with visual or neurological signs, need urgent assessment; never interpret a nosebleed as a treatment.
