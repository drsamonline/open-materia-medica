Oleander

Made from the oleander leaf — the garden shrub whose oleandrin is a genuine cardiac glycoside poison, and the classical remedy of the weeping skin and the involuntary stool.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a heart and a skin in concert: palpitations with a weak, irregular beat, an eczema of the scalp and behind the ears that weeps and crusts, and the books' odd pairing — involuntary stool passing with the urine — in a person worn thin by both. The weeping-skin-and-heart remedy: that is Oleander.

The substance, in plain words

Oleander is the beautiful, deadly garden shrub of the Mediterranean, its leaves carrying oleandrin — a genuine cardiac glycoside, kin to foxglove's chemistry, dangerous enough that a single leaf can poison a child. The proving gave the remedy its dual portrait of heart and weeping skin.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and sadness. Worry over the palpitations and the skin, gloom in the chronic states.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction and heat.
  • Forgetfulness. The books note a foggy, forgetful mind with the complaints.

The body

  • Palpitations, weak and irregular. The keynote: fluttering, irregular heart-action with a sense of weakness — the oleandrin shadow.
  • Eczema of scalp and ears. The weeping, crusted eczema of the scalp margin and behind the ears.
  • Involuntary stool with urine. The celebrated pairing — stool escapes as urine passes — the books' confirmatory sign.
  • Weakness after stool. Profound exhaustion following the involuntary evacuations.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night deepen the heart and the skin.
  • Better for rest and quiet. Rest and quiet steady the heart and the skin.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Heart and circulation, skin (weeping eczema of scalp), bowels, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The deadly shrub's proving gave the heart-and-weeping-skin portrait; the old books applied it to the palpitating, eczematous constitutions — and the plant's own glycoside chemistry explains the cardiac half of the picture.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Oleandrin is genuinely a cardiac glycoside — the same molecular family as digoxin — and oleander poisoning is a real, documented emergency in every warm region of the world, its heart-toxicity precisely the toxicology of the digitalis family. No drug came of this shrub (its chemistry is too dangerous); its portrait in the library is the poison's faithful mirror.
