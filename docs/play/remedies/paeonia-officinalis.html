Paeonia officinalis

Made from the peony — the garden flower whose root is a genuine astringent drug of the old dispensaries and whose paeoniflorin is actively researched today, and the classical remedy of the anal fissure.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the anus in torment: fissures that burn like fire with every stool and for hours after, offensive ulcerations about the anus, bleeding that smarts, a sensation of biting and burning between the stools, and the books' great anal remedy. The burning fissure: that is Paeonia.

The substance, in plain words

The peony is the garden flower of old physic gardens, its root an official astringent of the old dispensaries, prescribed for the same complaints the remedy serves. Its paeoniflorin is genuinely researched today for anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects — the flower's chemistry is real, ancient and active.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the pain. Dread of every stool, worse at night with the burning.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant smarting.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, recurrent fissure states.

The body

  • Anal fissures burning like fire. The keynote: fissures of the anus that burn like fire during and long after the stool — the books' most quoted use.
  • Offensive ulcerations. Ulcers about the anus, offensive, oozing, refusing to heal.
  • Biting and burning between stools. The quoted sensation — biting, burning, smarting even at rest.
  • Bleeding that smarts. Blood that burns as it passes — the books' confirmatory detail.
  • Worse stool and night. Every evacuation is a crisis; the nights burn long.
  • Better for rest. Rest between stools, and warmth, ease the burning.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Anus (fissures, ulcers), rectum, veins of the region, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The physic-garden astringent's official career — the old answer to the same complaint — gave the proving its frame; Paeonia became the classical remedy of the anal fissure, its burning-fire detail recorded across a century of dispensary use.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Peony root was a genuine, official astringent drug of the old dispensaries — the era's treatment for exactly these complaints — and its paeoniflorin is among traditional pharmacology's most-researched compounds, with real anti-inflammatory activity in modern studies. The fissure it portrayed is today managed with fibre, topical treatments and, when needed, surgery — the complaint is treatable and common.
