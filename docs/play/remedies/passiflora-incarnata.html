Passiflora incarnata

Made from the passion flower — the American vine whose modern extracts carry genuine calming evidence, and the classical remedy of the sleepless and the overwrought.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a mind that cannot dismount: sleeplessness from nervous excitement — worry, grief, overwork — with restless tossing, a nervousness that runs through the whole day, and the books' gentle great sedative. The sleepless-and-overwrought remedy: that is Passiflora.

The substance, in plain words

The passion flower is the exotic vine of the American south, its name from the Passion of Christ in the flower's structure. Its extracts carry a genuinely studied calming chemistry — chrysin and the flavonoids — with real, if modest, clinical evidence for anxiety and sleeplessness, and it was the Eclectic school's favourite sedative.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Sleeplessness from nervousness. The keynote: the mind cannot dismount — worry, grief, overwork keep the eyes open and the bed a torment.
  • Restlessness of the whole day. The nervousness runs through waking hours as well — the books' companion state.
  • Anxiety and fear. Worry over trifles, worse in the quiet hours of the night.

The body

  • Restless tossing in bed. Turning, twitching, unable to settle — the books describe the sleepless restlessness precisely.
  • Nervous exhaustion. The drained, over-wrought state after prolonged worry or work.
  • Headaches of nervousness. The congestive, nervous headache riding the sleeplessness.
  • Spasmodic nervous complaints. The books add it to the nervous cough and breathlessness.
  • Worse the quiet and the dark. Solitude and darkness deepen the sleeplessness.
  • Better for company and open air. Gentle company and fresh air ease the over-wrought nerve.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic sedative's proving gave the sleepless-and-overwrought portrait; Passiflora became the classical remedy of the mind that cannot dismount, standing at the gentle end of the sleep chapter.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Passionflower is one of herbal medicine's better-documented calmatives: standardised extracts have genuine clinical-trial evidence for anxiety and sleep disturbance, modest but real, and the plant remains a licensed ingredient in sleep preparations in Europe. The gentle sedative of the old school has kept a genuine, if quiet, modern career.
