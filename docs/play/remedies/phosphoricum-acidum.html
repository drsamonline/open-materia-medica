Acidum phosphoricum

Made from phosphoric acid — the mineral acid of the body's own energy chemistry, and the classical remedy of apathetic exhaustion from grief or growth.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a person emptied to apathy: grief or over-study or a growing spurt has drained the colour and the interest from life; the face is pale and drawn, the mind indifferent, the body heavy, hair falls in handfuls, and nothing — not even sadness — seems worth the effort. The apathetic depletion: that is Phosphoric acid.

The substance, in plain words

Phosphoric acid is the mineral acid at the heart of the body's energy chemistry — phosphate is the backbone of ATP, of bone, of every cell's ledger. As a drug it corrodes; as a dilution the old school used it for 'debility from nervous exhaustion'; the proving gave the remedy its portrait of drained, grey apathy.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Apathetic indifference. The keynote of the mind: indifferent to everything — joy, grief, business, love; the books' great apathy remedy.
  • Grief without tears. Home-sickness, unrequited love, loss — borne in grey silence, neither wept nor spoken.
  • Debility of over-study. The schoolboy and the clerk, worn to grey by the desk; the mind refuses its work.

The body

  • Depletion after grief or growth. The keynote: exhaustion after grief, after growing, after fevers — the whole system's reserves spent.
  • Hair falls in handfuls. The quoted sign — hair loss with the depletion.
  • Painlessness of complaints. The books note the absence of pain — things are wrong, but nothing hurts enough to matter.
  • Worse exertion and conversation. The least mental or physical effort deepens the grey; talking tires.
  • Better for rest and short sleep Rest, warmth and quiet slowly return a little colour.
  • Diarrhea of indifference. Painless morning diarrhea accompanies the apathetic state.

Where the classical picture comes from

The acid's proving gave the grey portrait — apathy, depletion, painless complaints, falling hair; the remedy attached to the grief-worn and the over-schooled of the era, whose exhaustion modern medicine would call burnout, depression and convalescence.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Phosphoric acid's real medicine is indirect but fundamental: phosphate chemistry is the body's energy economy (ATP), and genuine phosphate disorders — refeeding syndrome among them — are modern hospital medicine. The remedy's apathy is now psychiatry's depression and exhaustion's work-up; the acid itself is industrially vital and medically humble.
