Radium bromatum

Made from radium bromide — the radioactive salt; the classical remedy of the deep bone ache, the late-appearing ulcer and the burning skin.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the slow, deep action of radium: severe aching in the bones and joints that eases on moving, an ulcer that appears late and far from where the radium touched, skin that burns as if afire, and a great weariness with it all. Radium's slow, penetrating effect: that is Radium bromatum.

The substance, in plain words

Radium bromide, the radioactive salt, potentised. Boericke records the provings of Diffenbach with a preparation of high radioactivity, and the clinical use in rheumatism, gout, skin disease, nævi and cancers.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Apprehension and depression. Fear of being alone in the dark, with a great desire to be with people.
  • Tired and irritable. A weariness of mind as well as body.

The body

  • Deep aching in the bones and joints, worst at night and better on continued motion; the legs, arms and neck feel hard and brittle, as if they would break.
  • Skin. Erythema and dermatitis with itching, burning, swelling and redness; small pimples; ulcers and necrosis; itching as if the skin were afire.
  • Head. Occipital and vertex pain with lumbar aching; pain over the right eye spreading back to the occiput; violent trifacial neuralgia.
  • Stomach and abdomen. An empty, sinking feeling; violent cramps and much flatulence; pain over McBurney's point.
  • Urinary. Renal irritation, albuminuria and casts; nephritis with rheumatic symptoms.
  • Late appearance of symptoms; ulcers from radium burns heal very slowly.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

The bones and joints, the skin, the kidneys, and the blood.

Where the classical picture comes from

The proving of radium bromide by Diffenbach and the early clinical experience of radium burns. Radium's delayed, penetrating injury — the ulcer that appears far from the point of contact and long after it — is the keynote Boericke singles out, comparing it to Anacardium.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Radium is real medicine. Radium-223 (Xofigo) is an approved treatment for bone metastases in prostate cancer, delivering its radiation precisely to the bone; radium was one of the earliest cancer therapies, and radium-226 was used in luminous paint until the 'radium girls' of the 1920s sickened with anaemia, jaw necrosis and bone cancer — the very picture the proving describes. Radium is also, like X-ray, a cause of leukaemia and bone cancer.

Safety. Radium and radiation exposure are serious; seek specialist advice for any suspected exposure or an ulcer that will not heal. Unexplained bone pain, bruising or bleeding needs prompt medical assessment.
