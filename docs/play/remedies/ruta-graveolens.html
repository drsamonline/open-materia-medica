Ruta graveolens

Made from common rue — the bitter herb of Roman gardens whose rutin gave the bioflavonoid family its name, and the classical remedy of the periosteum and the wrist.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the overused wrist and the bruised bone-covering: lame, sore, deep-boned lameness after straining the wrists or ankles, aching in the bone-membranes, worse lying down in the evening, with a characteristic restlessness and low spirits. The bruised periosteum: that is Ruta.

The substance, in plain words

Rue is the bitter blue-leaved herb of old Roman gardens — once eaten as an antidote salad, now known for its rutin, the bioflavonoid studied for capillary strength. Its furocoumarin chemistry irritates the skin in sunlight; the proving gave the deep connective-tissue portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Low spirits and anxiety. Depressed, despondent about the lameness; fretful, worse in the evening.
  • Restlessness. Unable to find a comfortable position; general fidgetiness with the pains.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction and worry.

The body

  • Periosteal bruising. The keynote: soreness of the bone-membranes — shins, wrists, ankles — as if the bones had been beaten.
  • The strained wrist. Lameness of the wrists from overuse — writing, typing, lifting; the classical typist's and washerwoman's remedy.
  • Worse lying down. The evening and night bring the aching on; must move about.
  • Contracted, shortened tendons. The books use it where tendons shorten after injury — Dupuytren's-like states, flexed fingers.
  • Eye-strain. Ache in and around the eyes from fine work — the books' great eye-strain remedy.
  • Better from motion and warmth. Gentle movement and warm wraps ease the deep soreness.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Periosteum and bone membranes, tendons and wrists, eyes (strain), joints.

Where the classical picture comes from

The herb's deep-tissue irritation in proving matched the washerwomen and scribes the old clinicians saw — overused wrists, bruised shins, sore bone-membranes; Ruta became the remedy of strained connective tissue.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Rutin — rue's namesake flavonoid — is a real bioflavonoid studied for capillary fragility and venous insufficiency, and sold as a supplement for exactly the vein-and-capillary fragility the books hint at. The herb's coumarin chemistry is a genuine photosensitiser; the plant is a real, working pharmacology, half useful, half hazard.
