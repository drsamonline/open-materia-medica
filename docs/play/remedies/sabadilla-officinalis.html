Sabadilla officinalis

Made from cevadilla seed — the veratrine-bearing seed once used as an insecticide powder, and the classical remedy of the sneezing paroxysm.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of sneezing that takes over: fits of sneezing on a single trigger — dust, perfume, the smell of food — with an itchy, crawling nose, watery eyes, and a sense that a single grain of dust could ignite the whole face. The sneezing paroxysm, the itchy nose: that is Sabadilla.

The substance, in plain words

Cevadilla is the lily-family seed of the New World, rich in veratrine-like alkaloids — the same family as white hellebore's blood-pressure chemistry. Ground as a powder it was a genuine insecticide of the old apothecary; the proving gave the classical hay-fever portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and nervousness. Wired, startled, worse as the sneezing climbs; vertigo with the fits.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from the smells that trigger the attacks.
  • Delusions of grandeur Moods swing — gay then mournful — with the coryza's course.

The body

  • Sneezing paroxysms. The keynote: fits of continuous sneezing, often from one trigger — dust, odour, looking at light.
  • Itchy crawling nose. Itching and tingling in the nose and soft palate, as if a grain of dust were lodged.
  • Watery eyes and nose. Profuse watering in the paroxysm, between which all is quiet.
  • Worse smell of food. The kitchen sets the fits off; the books note hunger with aversion to eating.
  • Better warmth and food. Warm food and drink relieve — the reverse of Allium's air-longing.
  • Sore throat with stitching. Raw stitching pain on swallowing, worse empty swallowing.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Nose and sneeze-centre, throat, eyes, mood and nerves.

Where the classical picture comes from

The insecticide seed's irritation of the nose and throat gave the proving its portrait; clinically Sabadilla attached to the hay-fever sufferer whose sneezes came in triggered volleys, and whose palate itched for weeks.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Cevadilla's veratrine alkaloids are real, potent chemistry — genuinely insecticidal, genuinely toxic to mammals at crude dose, cousins of the hellebore blood-pressure story. The seed's modern life is pesticidal rather than medicinal; the sneezing portrait it left in the library is its gentler legacy.

Safety. Sneezing with wheeze or breathlessness suggests asthma — needs medical assessment; chronic hay fever deserves proper treatment.
