Sabina officinalis

Made from savin — the juniper of old apothecary gardens whose oil was the infamous abortifacient of the centuries, and the classical remedy of threatened miscarriage.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a bleeding that threatens a pregnancy: bright-red blood with clots, pains dragging from the small of the back through to the pubic bone, a uterus that will not hold, and — the strange keynote — aggravation from music. The threatened miscarriage: that is Sabina.

The substance, in plain words

Savin is the juniper shrub of apothecary gardens, its volatile oil a genuine, dangerous uterine stimulant — the infamous abortifacient of centuries of desperate women, recorded in every pharmacopoeia as a poison. The proving gave the remedy its portrait: bright bleeding, dragging pains, music-worse.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and fear. Terror of losing the pregnancy, restlessness with the pains.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction and from noise.
  • Music aggravates. The celebrated keynote: music sets off the symptoms — the only major remedy with this sign.

The body

  • Bright-red bleeding with clots. The keynote: uterine haemorrhage, scarlet with clots, threatening miscarriage or flooding.
  • Sacrum-to-pubis pains. Pains dragging from the small of the back, through the pelvis, to the pubic bone — the books' path of threatened abortion.
  • Uterus that will not hold. Habitual tendency to miscarry at the same week each time.
  • Inflammation after miscarriage. The books use it in the febrile, painful aftermath of loss.
  • Worse music and motion. Both aggravate — the odd pairing the proving recorded.
  • Better for rest and quiet. Rest and quiet, and the bleeding's cessation, bring ease.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Uterus (threatened miscarriage, flooding), pelvis and sacrum, circulation.

Where the classical picture comes from

The abortifacient oil's real, terrible career — recorded in coroners' reports and pharmacopoeia warnings alike — gave the proving its frame; the remedy became the classical answer to the threatened miscarriage, its music-worse keynote unmatched in the library.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Savin oil's history is pharmacology's darker ledger: a genuine, effective, dangerous uterine stimulant that filled poison records for centuries, retired when safe obstetrics arrived. The miscarriages it addressed are today managed with progesterone, cervical cerclage and skilled antenatal care — medicine's answer to the remedy's prayer.
