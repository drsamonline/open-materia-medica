Senecio aureus

Made from golden ragwort — the American meadow herb of the Eclectic female regulators, an official uterine drug, and the classical remedy of the delayed and suppressed menses.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a cycle that will not come: delayed, scanty, suppressed menses — from cold, from emotion, from travel — with the bearing-down and the nervous irritation that accompany the arrested flow; the books' great delayed-menses remedy. The delayed-flow regulator: that is Senecio.

The substance, in plain words

Golden ragwort is the yellow meadow herb of North America, its root an official 'female regulator' of the Eclectic school and the old dispensaries — genuinely used to bring on the arrested menses. Its pyrrolizidine alkaloids are genuinely active — and genuinely hepatotoxic, the modern warning. The proving gave the remedy its delayed-cycle portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability and nervousness. Cross, fretful, worse around the cycle; the books pair it with the nervous, delayed-menses temperament.
  • Anxiety and fear. Worry over the delayed flow, the bearing-down.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, arrested states.

The body

  • Delayed, suppressed menses. The keynote: the flow delayed by cold, wet feet, emotion, travel — the books' great emmenagogue remedy.
  • Bearing-down with it. The dragging pelvic sensation accompanying the arrested cycle.
  • Nervous irritation of the cycle. Restlessness, irritability, the sleeplessness before the flow.
  • Worse cold damp and emotion. Wet weather and emotional shocks arrest the flow further.
  • Suppression from wet feet The old books record the flow suppressed by getting chilled.
  • Better for warmth and the flow's establishment. Warmth, and the flow at last arriving, ease everything.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic female-regulator's official career gave the proving its frame; Senecio became the classical remedy of the delayed and suppressed menses, read in the era when the arrested cycle was the complaint of every women's dispensary.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Senecio was a genuine, official female regulator of the Eclectic school — its chemistry really acted on the cycle — and its pyrrolizidine alkaloids carry a genuine, modern hepatotoxicity warning, documented. The delayed cycles it managed are now gynaecology's work-up: pregnancy, thyroid, prolactin and the hormonal causes all tested and treated directly. The herb is real; the modern answer is diagnosis.
