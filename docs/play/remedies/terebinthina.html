Terebinthina

Made from turpentine — the pine-resin distillate of the antiseptic century, whose renal toxicology is genuine, and the classical remedy of the congested, tympanitic bladder.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a bladder affecting the whole system: burning, scalding, bloody urine with mucus and gravel; a belly bloating with tympanitic gas; drowsiness and confusion drifting in; and the sense that the kidneys and bladder are inflamed together. The systemic, tympanitic cystitis: that is Terebinthina.

The substance, in plain words

Turpentine is the pine-resin distillate of the old pharmacy — antiseptic, counter-irritant, abortifacient, and genuinely renal-toxic in overdose, its albuminuric poisonings recorded carefully in the nineteenth century. The proving gave the remedy its congested-system portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Drowsiness and confusion. The keynote of the mind: heavy, stupid, drifting — the systemic, uremic-shade picture of the books.
  • Anxiety with the burning. Fear during the scalding, the bloody passages.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise and light.

The body

  • Bloody, burning urine. The keynote: urine burning, scalding, bloody, with mucus and gravel — the inflammatory kidney-and-bladder picture.
  • Tympanitic distension. The abdomen balloons with gas that will not pass — the books' striking association.
  • Drowsy, systemic states. The drowsiness, the wandering mind — the era's portrait of the poisoned, congested system.
  • Albuminuria picture. The books note the kidney involvement — the turpentine-poisoning legacy in the portrait.
  • Worse damp cold and night. Wet weather and the night hours aggravate everything.
  • Better for rest Rest and warmth steady the system.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Kidneys and bladder, abdomen (tympanites), blood and systemic states, urine.

Where the classical picture comes from

The turpentine poisonings of the industrial era — albuminuria, drowsiness, tympanites — wrote the proving before it was conducted; the remedy became the classical portrait of the bladder affecting the whole system.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Turpentine's real pharmacology is double: genuinely antiseptic — it cleaned wounds and preserved the centuries — and genuinely nephrotoxic, its renal poisonings a fixture of old toxicology. Modern paint chemistry retired it from medicine; the cystitis it portrayed is now culture-and-antibiotics medicine, and the drowsy systemic states it feared are the sepsis work-ups of modern emergency rooms.
