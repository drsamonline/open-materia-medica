Theridion curassavicum

Made from the orange spider of the Antilles — the tropic tree-spider whose venom is a genuine ion-channel research subject, and the classical remedy of the noise-and-motion vertigo.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of vertigo with every sense a trigger: dizziness and nausea at the least motion of the head, at the least noise, worse closing the eyes (the world spins when the lids shut), the noise of a conversation setting off the swaying — and the books' great vertigo remedy of the sensitive constitution. The vertigo-of-every-trigger: that is Theridion.

The substance, in plain words

The orange tree-spider of the Antilles is a genuine venom-bearer — its peptides, like all spider venoms, act on the nervous system's ion channels and are studied in modern pharmacology. The proving gave the remedy its celebrated vertigo portrait: motion, noise and closed eyes each a trigger.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the vertigo. Fear of the next attack, of falling; worse in the quiet after the noise.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise — which also brings the vertigo.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, recurrent states.

The body

  • Vertigo at the least motion. The keynote: dizziness from the slightest motion of the head or body — the books' great motion-vertigo remedy.
  • Worse closing the eyes. The celebrated modality — closing the eyes worsens the vertigo, the world spinning in the dark.
  • Worse noise. The sound of a conversation or a footfall sets off the swaying and the nausea.
  • Nausea and faintness with the vertigo. The seasick-sick nausea riding the dizziness.
  • Worse motion and jar. Carriages, jolts, and sudden movements renew the spinning.
  • Better for rest and quiet. Stillness, quiet and open eyes bring the only steadying.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Balance and vertigo, senses (noise-sensitivity), stomach (nausea), nervous system.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Antilles tree-spider's proving gave the library its most specific vertigo portrait — the closed-eye, noise-triggered spinning; Theridion became the remedy of the sensitive, swaying constitution, standing in the spider chapter beside Tarentula's rhythm.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Spider venoms are a genuine frontier of pharmacology — ion-channel-modulating peptides studied for pain, rhythm and neuromuscular disease — and the orange spider's kin share that real research life. Motion-triggered vertigo is now vestibular medicine's: the Epley manoeuvre, vestibular rehabilitation and diagnosis by examination. The venom is researched; the vertigo is treatable.
