Xerophyllum

Made from the bear grass of the American mountains — the drought-proof lily of the high slopes, and the classical remedy of the obstinate psoriasis.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the psoriatic chapter's most obstinate member: the thickened, scaling skin that resists every remedy, the books' great bear-grass remedy of the mountain chapter, and the drought-proof lily of the high American slopes. The obstinate-psoriasis lily: that is Xerophyllum.

The substance, in plain words

The bear grass is the drought-proof lily of the high American slopes, its tough basal leaves woven into baskets by the Indigenous peoples — and its rhizome a genuine Eclectic remedy of the chronic-skin shelf, used for the obstinate psoriasis across the old American school. The proving gave the lily the obstinate-psoriasis portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and despondency. The misery of the obstinate, chronic skin — worse at night, worse in the winter.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant itching and the chronicity.
  • Despair of recovery. The books record the hopelessness of the treatment-resistant skin.

The body

  • Obstinate, thickened psoriasis. The keynote: the thickened, scaling, treatment-resistant psoriasis of the books' mountain chapter.
  • Chronic, unyielding skin. The chronic skin that resists the whole shelf of the old books.
  • Worse cold damp and winter. Wet weather and the winter thicken the skin further.
  • Better for dry weather and the lily's course. Dry weather and the slow course of the mountain remedy ease the bear grass.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Skin (obstinate psoriasis, thickening), general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The mountain lily's proving gave the obstinate-psoriasis portrait; Xerophyllum became the classical remedy of the treatment-resistant skin — the drought-proof bear grass serving the chapter that resisted everything else.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

The bear grass's real career is the basket-weave of the Indigenous peoples and the toughness of the high slopes — its medical career an Eclectic footnote of the psoriasis chapter. The obstinate psoriasis it portrayed is now dermatology's strongest success story: biologics have transformed what resisted everything; the lily's portrait is the record of what the old shelf could not cure.
