Agaricus muscarius

Made from fly agaric — the white-spotted toadstool of fairy tales whose muscimol is a genuine deliriant, and the classical remedy of twitching, chorea and winter chilblains.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a nervous system that cannot hold still: constant twitching and jerking of single muscles, chorea-like movements that come and go, eyelids that dance, chilblains that burn and itch in the cold, vertigo with the sensation of falling, and the books' great winter-and-twitching remedy. The twitching toadstool: that is Agaricus.

The substance, in plain words

The fly agaric is the red-and-white toadstool of every fairy tale, its muscimol and ibotenic acid a genuine deliriant chemistry — GABA-active, reality-bending, documented from Siberian ritual to modern toxicology. The proving gave the remedy its portrait of the restless, twitching, winter-afflicted nervous system.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Fear and anxiety. Fear of death, of falling, of being alone; anxious restlessness with the twitching.
  • Delirium of the mushroom. The proving echoes the poison: confused states, singing, talking, fixed ideas.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from cold and from contradiction.

The body

  • Twitching and jerking. The keynote: constant twitching of single muscles — eyelids, corners of the mouth, fingers; chorea-like movements.
  • Chilblains of winter. Burning, itching, ulcerated chilblains on fingers, toes, ears in the cold season — the books' great association.
  • Vertigo with falling sensation. Vertigo with the sensation of falling forward, worse closing the eyes
  • Worse cold air. Cold and cold weather aggravate the twitching and the chilblains; warmth indoors paradoxically
  • Spasmodic complaints. Hiccups, yawning, choreiform jerks of children — the books' spasmodic chapter.
  • Better for warmth Warmth and quiet ease the twitching.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Nervous system (twitching, chorea), skin (chilblains), balance, general spasm.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Siberian deliriant mushroom's proving gave the twitching, jerking, winter-chilblain portrait; Agaricus became the classical remedy of the restless muscle and the cold-season skin, read beside the toadstool's genuine psychopharmacology.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Muscimol is genuinely psychoactive — a GABA-receptor agonist studied in neuroscience for decades, from the shamans' visions to the molecular pharmacology laboratory. The mushroom is a real deliriant, real toxicology, real research subject; its medical career is its poison file, and its portrait in the library is its nervous system's mirror.
