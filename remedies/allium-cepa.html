Allium cepa

Made from the common onion — the kitchen bulb whose tear-gas chemistry is real pharmacology, and the classical hay-fever remedy of burning coryza and bland tears.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a hay fever with a chemical signature: a burning, smarting, watery nasal flow that reddens the upper lip, eyes streaming but bland and comfortable — the exact reverse of what the onion does to the cook's eyes — worse in warm rooms, better in open air. Burning nose, bland tears: that is Allium cepa.

The substance, in plain words

The onion is the world's most-used vegetable and a genuine chemical curiosity: cutting it releases syn-propanethial-S-oxide, the volatile tear-gas of every kitchen — real, measured lachrymatory pharmacology. The proving simply turned the onion's chemistry upon itself: burning coryza, bland eyes, better air.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability from the symptoms. Cross, worse in the evening as the room warms; anxious about the constant drip.
  • Low spirits indoors. The mood improves the moment fresh air is reached.
  • Restlessness with the burning. Cannot settle with the smarting nose and the sneezing fits.

The body

  • Burning nasal flow, bland tears. The keynote: profuse, burning, acrid nasal discharge scalding the lip, while the eye-water is bland — the reverse of Euphrasia.
  • Sneezing fits. Continuous sneezing on entering a warm room, in the evening.
  • Worse warm rooms. Every catarrh worsens indoors; the sufferer longs for the door.
  • Better open air. Cool fresh air relieves the nose within minutes.
  • Cough from the tickle. A hacking cough from the raw larynx, worse cold damp rooms.
  • Thirstlessness and head-dullness. Little thirst; a dull, dragged-down feeling with the coryza.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Nose and eyes (coryza, hay fever), larynx, respiratory mucosa, senses.

Where the classical picture comes from

The onion's tear chemistry is the proving: a bulb that makes eyes weep and noses burn produced the exact portrait — with the eye-water curiously bland, as if the chemistry mocked its own cook. The remedy became the classical hay-fever standby.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Onion chemistry is genuinely pharmacological: the lachrymator is a real, studied volatile compound; quercetin, the onion's flavonoid, is researched for antihistamine-like effects and circulatory benefits. The kitchen's oldest tear-jerker is a small pharmacy of actives — folk wisdom's most testable claim.

Safety. Facial pain with fever, one-sided purulent discharge, or hay fever that wrecks sleep and work deserve medical treatment — modern antihistamines are safe and effective.
