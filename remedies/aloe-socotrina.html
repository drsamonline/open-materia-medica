Aloe socotrina

Made from Socotra aloe — the desert lily whose dried latex is a real stimulant laxative, and the classical remedy of the unreliable rectum.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a rectum that will not be trusted: a constant bearing-down and urging as if the stool will escape the moment it is not attended, early-morning urgency driving the person out of bed, rumbling gurgling fermentation, and an insecure, ' hurry-to-the-closet' life. Bearing-down urgency, morning-driven: that is Aloe.

The substance, in plain words

Aloe latex is the dried juice of the desert lily — a genuine, effective stimulant laxative that works by irritating the bowel's own nerves, still sold as a real medicine. The proving and the purgative action are the same chemistry at different doses.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritable hurry. Cross, hurried, worse in the morning rush with the bowel urgency.
  • Indifference. Low spirits, aversion to company during the chronic looseness.
  • Anxiety about the bowels. Fear of accidents, of being caught away from home.

The body

  • Bearing-down rectal urgency. The keynote: a constant sensation of fullness and downward pressure in the rectum, with insecurity about the sphincter.
  • Early morning urgency. Stool drives the person up at five or six in the morning, worse immediately after eating and drinking.
  • Rumbling fermentation. Loud gurgling, gas, and a hot, heavy, full abdomen after meals.
  • Loss of control. The books describe involuntary passage and the dread of it, in children and the aged alike.
  • Worse heat. Hot weather and the warmth of the bed aggravate; the summer diarrhoea remedy.
  • Better cold applications. Cool air and cold cloths to the abdomen ease the urging.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Rectum and sphincter, bowel, abdomen (fermentation), digestion.

Where the classical picture comes from

The latex's purgative chemistry produced exactly the remedy's picture — bearing-down, morning urgency, sphincter insecurity — and the remedy served the chronic 'laxative-taken' bowel of the Victorian era, whose aloe habit was notorious.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Aloe latex is a real stimulant laxative of the anthraquinone family — effective, and responsible enough that modern regulators limit chronic use for fear of dependence and electrolyte loss. The plant is genuine pharmacology: a working drug whose classical portrait is its own over-dose.
