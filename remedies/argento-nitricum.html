Argentum nitricum

Made from silver nitrate — the caustic silver salt that still sits in every emergency department, and the classical remedy of anticipation and impulsive hurry.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of hurry in the nerves: the person who cannot wait — walks fast, talks fast, decides in a flash and regrets at leisure — dreads the bridge, the height, the closed room; craves sugar and salt; has diarrhea from pure anticipation, and strange vertigo in high places. Anticipatory hurry, sugar-craving, fear of heights: that is Argentum nitricum.

The substance, in plain words

Silver nitrate is the real drug of emergency rooms: the black-stick causter that stops nosebleeds, the drops that protect newborn eyes, the cauteriser of warts and wounds. The proving of this same salt gave the classical portrait of nerves stretched to snapping by anticipation.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anticipatory anxiety with hurry. The keynote: dread of the coming event with restless impatience — like Gelsemium but electric rather than heavy; cannot sit still for thinking of it.
  • Impulsiveness. Acts on the sudden impulse, says the rash thing, walks into the risky situation — then trembles.
  • Fears and phobias. Fear of heights, of bridges, of passing a corner where people are gathered, of closed spaces; agoraphobia's ancestor.
  • Time obsession. Strange dread that time moves too slowly; arrives everywhere absurdly early.

The body

  • Anticipatory diarrhea. The classic: loose stool before the examination, the stage, the wedding — nervous diarrhea in the truest sense.
  • Cravings. Intense desire for sugar, which disagrees; also salt; wine aggravates.
  • Vertigo of heights. Dizziness and fear at edges and heights; the world tilts near the window.
  • Better for belching and eructation. The stomach and nerves ease with gas coming up — the quoted gastric keynote.
  • Headaches with eye-strain. Headaches from close work, better pressure and cool air.
  • Worse warmth and sweets. Hot rooms and sugar set both nerves and stomach off.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Nerves and emotions, digestion (anticipatory), head and eyes, general reactivity.

Where the classical picture comes from

The caustic salt's proving produced the hurried, anticipatory, phobic portrait; clinically Argentum-nit attached to performers, examinees and the impulsive-anxious constitution — the portrait of nerves racing ahead of the body.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Silver nitrate is a genuine emergency drug to this day: neonatal eye prophylaxis, nosebleed cautery, wound and wart cauterisation — its caustic precision is real pharmacology. Its other side, argyria, the irreversible blue-grey skin of chronic silver overuse, is textbook toxicology. The salt is a true two-faced drug.
