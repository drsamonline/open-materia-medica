Arnica montana

Made from leopard's bane — the alpine flower of mountain pastures, whose real bruise-healing chemistry survives in modern ointments, and the classical first remedy of every accident.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the person who says 'I'm fine' while leaning on a lamppost: bruised, sore, lame after a fall or a blow, everything hurts and the bed feels too hard, yet they deny anything is wrong and send the doctor away. The bruised denial: that is Arnica.

The substance, in plain words

Arnica is the golden alpine flower of European mountain pastures. Its sesquiterpene lactones are genuinely anti-inflammatory — arnica ointment is a real, studied topical for bruises and sprains, though the plant itself is toxic if swallowed.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Denial of illness. The keynote of the mind: says nothing is wrong, refuses examination, insists on getting up — the well-known 'Arnica state' after accidents.
  • Fear of being touched. Dreads the approach of anyone who might examine the hurt.
  • Irritability. Cross, answers briefly, worse from consolation

The body

  • Bruised soreness. The keynote: everything sore, bruised, beaten — the whole body feels as if pounded.
  • The bed feels too hard. The celebrated sign: no position is comfortable, the mattress seems made of stones.
  • Worse least touch and motion. The part cannot bear touch, pressure, or the least movement; yet better lying still with the head low.
  • After injuries and operations. The first remedy of the old surgical books — falls, blows, sprains, surgical shock, labour.
  • Black eye and extravasation. Bruises that turn black and blue; the books use it wherever blood has leaked into tissue.
  • Fever from trauma. The hot, dry, bruised fever after injury — with the same denial.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Whole body after trauma, muscles and blood vessels (bruising), skin, surgical states.

Where the classical picture comes from

The alpine flower's bruise-healing reputation is ancient European mountain medicine; the proving gave the denial-and-soreness portrait, and Arnica became the first remedy of every accident — the alphabet's first letter in the old acute prescribing.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Arnica's topical chemistry is genuinely effective: randomised trials support arnica gel for bruising after injuries and cosmetic surgery, and it remains a mainstream pharmacy product across Europe. The flower's sesquiterpene lactones are real anti-inflammatory agents — one of the library's clearest bridges from folk remedy to studied drug.

Safety. Head injury with vomiting, confusion or drowsiness is an emergency; serious sprains, suspected fractures and post-operative problems need medical care.
