Bacillinum

The classical nosode prepared from the tubercle bacillus — the twin of Tuberculinum of the consumptive era, and the classical remedy of the ringworm-and-adenoid child.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the child marked by the family disease: ringworm that comes and goes with the chest, adenoids and tonsils that never settle, the dry night cough of the consumptive tendency, the history of the family complaint — the books' second great tubercular nosode. The family-marked child: that is Bacillinum.

The substance, in plain words

Bacillinum is prepared from the tubercle bacillus itself — a nosode twin of Tuberculinum, made in the era when tuberculosis marked whole families across generations. Its preparation belongs to the consumptive century; the remedy is the family-history portrait of the books' great white plague.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and irritability. Cross, worried, worse at night; the books paint the nervous, marked child.
  • Restlessness. The tossing restlessness of the night cough.
  • Despondency. The family's shadow in the chronic states.

The body

  • Ringworm with the chest. The keynote: ringworm of the scalp that appears and vanishes with the chest complaints — the books' celebrated association.
  • Adenoids and tonsils. The swollen adenoids and recurrent tonsillitis of the tubercular diathesis.
  • Dry night cough. The harassing, dry cough of the night hours of the consumptive tendency.
  • Family history. The books take the family history of the great white plague as the ground of the case.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and the night own the cough and the glands.
  • Better for open air and the sea. Fresh air, mountain air and the sea ease the marked constitution.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Chest and lungs, glands (adenoids, tonsils), skin (ringworm), general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The consumptive century's second nosode; Bacillinum became the classical remedy of the family-marked child — ringworm riding with the chest, adenoids that never settle, the dry night cough — the tubercular diathesis read from the family tree.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Tuberculosis is the real story: the great white plague that marked families for centuries was met by BCG vaccine in 1921, antibiotics from 1943, and public-health measures that drove it from the leading killer of young adults to a curable, preventable disease. The ringworm and adenoids it accompanied are now dermatology's antifungals and the ENT clinic — the family mark has a modern answer.
