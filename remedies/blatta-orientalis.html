Blatta orientalis

Made from the oriental cockroach — the most unusual source in the library, long used in respiratory tradition, and a subject of genuine clinical research in respiratory medicine.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of persistent bronchial asthma with a history: the cough and breathlessness of the bronchitic, worse in damp and cold weather, better in warm dry air, with a susceptibility the old books traced to the 'roach constitution.' Damp-weather asthma, the bronchitic diathesis: that is Blatta.

The substance, in plain words

The oriental cockroach is the kitchen pest of warm climates — improbable as a medicine, yet its preparation has a documented history in respiratory tradition going back to Pliny's era, when roaches were used in chest complaints. Modern homeopathic researchers have published clinical series on roach preparations in asthma, making it one of the most-studied unusual sources.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Despondency of the chronic asthmatic. Gloom, anxiety about the next attack, worse in damp spells.
  • Irritability. Crossness with the constant chest oppression.
  • Anxiety at night. Dread of the night hours when the breath shortens.

The body

  • Bronchitic asthma diathesis. The keynote of use: persistent bronchial asthma and chronic bronchitis, worse damp cold, better dry warmth.
  • Worse damp and cold. Rain, fog and winter set the wheezing off.
  • Copious mucus. Profuse expectoration of the bronchitic chest.
  • Better warm dry air. Warm dry climates and rooms ease the breathing.
  • Weakness after attacks. Prostration following the paroxysms.
  • Ailments from damp dwellings. The books associate it with the housing of damp and crowded rooms.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Bronchi and lungs (chronic bronchitis, asthma), mucous membranes, vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The roach's use in chest complaints predates homeopathy by eighteen centuries — Pliny records it; the old Anglo-Indian tradition preserved it; the proving formalised the damp-weather bronchitic portrait that modern clinical researchers have published on.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Blatta is a genuine anomaly worth honesty: its preparation has real published clinical research in respiratory medicine, mostly within homeopathic journals — more than most unusual sources can claim. Cockroach allergen, paradoxically, is also a documented asthma trigger in urban housing (real immunology), a strange mirror of its remedy use.
