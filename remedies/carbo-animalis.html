Carbo animalis

Made from animal charcoal — the black decolorant of the old sugar refineries and pharmacies, and the classical remedy of the hard glands and the cancer-state portrait.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of glands that turn to stone: hard, indurated glands — cervical, axillary, inguinal — in a person worn thin and purple; the old books' 'cancerous diathesis' portrait; and the slow, stony hardening that resisted every treatment of the era. The stony-gland remedy of the old books: that is Carbo animalis.

The substance, in plain words

Animal charcoal is carbon from bone — the decolorant of the sugar refinery and the poison-absorber of the old pharmacy, cousin to vegetable charcoal's activated chemistry. The proving gave the remedy its portrait of stony glands and the depleted, purple, cachectic constitution of the era's gravest cases.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and despondency. Fear of the hardening, of the diagnosis no one says aloud; gloom deepening at night.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from touch and from contradiction.
  • Apathy. A dark indifference in the advanced states — the books record it with gravity.

The body

  • Stony indurated glands. The keynote: glands that turn hard as stone — neck, armpit, groin — and stay.
  • Worn thin and purple. The cachectic portrait — thin, sallow-purple, exhausted, the old books' 'cancerous diathesis.'
  • Old ulcers with hard edges. Chronic ulcers, indurated at their margins, refusing all healing.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night deepen the pains of the hardening.
  • Bleeding dark and offensive The books add dark, offensive bleeding to the advanced portrait.
  • Better for rest and warmth Rest and warmth ease the exhausted frame between the pains.

Where the classical picture comes from

The bone-black's proving gave the remedy its gravest portrait: the stony gland, the purple cachexia, the hard-edged ulcer. Carbo animalis became the classical remedy of the era's most feared states — used, at the end of everything else, when medicine had nothing left.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Animal charcoal's real chemistry is the same adsorptive physics as its vegetable cousin — decolorising, adsorbing, inert. Its remedy's territory is now oncology: the stony glands and cachectic states it portrayed are cancer medicine's today — imaged, biopsied, staged and treated, with real, improving outcomes. The era it belonged to is precisely the era before oncology existed.
