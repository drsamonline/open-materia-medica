Cedron

Made from cedron seed — the South American fever-berry of the old anti-malarial trade, and the classical remedy of the clockwork periodicity.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a fever that keeps perfect time: attacks that arrive with the punctuality of a clock — every day at the same hour, every other day, every week — with chills, heat and sweat each on schedule; and neuralgias that do the same, returning at the same hour each day. The clockwork periodicity: that is Cedron.

The substance, in plain words

Cedron — the seed of a South American Simaba — was exported to Europe in the fever era as a genuine anti-periodic, trading in the same century as Peruvian bark. Its fever-breaking reputation was real enough for the pharmacopoeia's notice; the proving gave the remedy its celebrated clockwork portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety before the attack. The dread of the hour's approach — the books paint the clock-watching sufferer.
  • Irritability. Cross during the intervals, worse as the hour nears.
  • Despondency. Gloom at the unbroken regularity of the returning paroxysms.

The body

  • Clockwork periodicity. The keynote: fevers and neuralgias that return at exactly the same hour — daily, alternate days, weekly — with uncanny punctuality.
  • Chill, heat, sweat on schedule. The classic three-stage paroxysm, each stage on time — the books' anti-periodic portrait.
  • Neuralgias by the clock. Pains that keep their own timetable — the same hour daily, the books' celebrated observation.
  • Worse at the paroxysm's hour. Nothing prevents the return; the sufferer waits for it.
  • Chilliness between attacks. Cold hands and feet in the intervals.
  • Better for warmth Warmth and rest between the hours ease the intervals.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Fever mechanism and periodicity, nerves (timed neuralgias), general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The fever-berry of the anti-periodic trade gave the proving its frame; Cedron became the classical remedy of the clockwork paroxysm — the anti-malarial era's portrait of the fever that keeps perfect time, traded beside the bark that conquered it.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Cedron's real career was the anti-periodic trade of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries — a genuine competitor of Peruvian bark in the fever markets, since retired. The periodic fevers it portrayed were malaria, and the bark it traded beside gave medicine quinine — the first specific drug; the clockwork fevers are now diagnosed by smear and cured by modern antimalarials.
