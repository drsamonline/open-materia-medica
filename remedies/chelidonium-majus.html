Chelidonium majus

Made from greater celandine — the yellow-latex herb of European walls whose alkaloids remain active subjects of pharmacological research.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the liver speaking through the back: pain under the right ribs that shoots straight to the right shoulder blade, a yellow tinge to skin and eyes, a thickly coated yellow tongue, and — the surprising keynote — relief from eating a little and from hot drinks. Right scapula pain, jaundice, better for food: that is Chelidonium.

The substance, in plain words

Greater celandine is the wall-side herb whose broken stem bleeds orange latex — the alkaloid signature of the poppy family it belongs to. Chelidonine, chelerythrine and sanguinarine are genuinely bioactive alkaloids, still researched for liver, antimicrobial and anti-tumour effects.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Cross indifference. Irritable, gloomy, averse to company; the books paint a resigned bilious temperament.
  • Anxiety with liver states. Fear of sickness, fixed on the right-sided pain.
  • Dullness. Slow thinking with the coated tongue and the jaundiced heaviness.

The body

  • Pain to the right scapula. The keynote: hepatic pain boring through to the right shoulder blade — a path so characteristic the old books made it a signature.
  • Jaundice picture. Yellow skin and eyes, pale or clay stool, dark urine, itching skin.
  • Coated yellow tongue. Thick yellow coating, teeth-marks at the edges, bitter taste.
  • Better for eating. The famous amelioration: the stomach and liver pains ease after eating, unlike almost every other gastric remedy.
  • Worse right side. Everything right: right hypochondrium, right shoulder, right side of the tongue.
  • Worse motion and touch. The liver region cannot bear touch or jarring; must move carefully.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Liver and gall-bladder, right shoulder region, digestion, bile ducts.

Where the classical picture comes from

The latex alkaloids proved on the liver region: right-sided pain, yellow coating, jaundice. Clinically Chelidonium became the remedy of the bilious, jaundiced, right-scapula-pained patient — the portrait of every 'liverish' constitution of the era.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Celandine's alkaloids are real pharmacology under study: chelerythrine shows antimicrobial and anti-cancer activity in laboratory models, and the herb persists in European phytomedicine for biliary complaints — though its liver toxicity at crude doses is also documented, a genuine warning. The remedy's source is a two-edged plant, exactly as the portrait suggests.
