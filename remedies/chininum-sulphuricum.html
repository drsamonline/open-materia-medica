Chininum sulphuricum

Made from quinine sulfate — the bark salt that conquered malaria and taught medicine cinchonism, and the classical remedy of the periodic fever with the ringing ear.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the bark's own signature in remedy form: periodic fevers returning on schedule, violent ringing and roaring in the ears, the deafness of the quinine, the congested head and the pale, bloated face — the books' great anti-periodic beside China. The ringing-ear periodic fever: that is Chininum sulphuricum.

The substance, in plain words

Quinine sulfate is the bark's own salt — the first specific drug in medical history, isolated from Peruvian bark, which conquered malaria and taught medicine the word 'cinchonism': the ringing ears, the deafness, the headache of its own overdose. The proving gave the remedy the overdose's portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and fear. The dread of the returning paroxysm, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise — which the ears already ring with.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, periodic states.

The body

  • Periodic fevers. The keynote: fevers returning on schedule — the bark's own territory, in remedy form.
  • Ringing, roaring ears. The celebrated keynote: violent tinnitus with the deafness of the overdose — the cinchonism portrait.
  • Congested, throbbing head. The headache of the bark's excess, congestive and throbbing.
  • Pale, bloated face. The books describe the pale, puffy face of the chronic quinine patient.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night deepen the fevers and the ringing.
  • Better for rest and quiet. Rest and quiet ease the ringing and the head.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

The bark salt's proving gave the overdose's portrait — ringing ears, periodic fevers, the pale bloated face; Chininum sulphuricum became the classical remedy of the bark's own side-effects, the anti-periodic's mirror.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Quinine's real medicine is one of history's greatest: the conquest of malaria, the founding of chemotherapy — the very idea that a specific chemical cures a specific disease. Cinchonism remains textbook toxicology, and the bark's descendants, the artemisinins, continue the conquest today. The salt's career spans four centuries of real pharmacology.
