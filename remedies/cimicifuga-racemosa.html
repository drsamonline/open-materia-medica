Cimicifuga racemosa

Made from black cohosh — the American forest herb that became a real menopause medicine, and the classical remedy of the gloomy, rheumatic woman.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a dark cloud over a suffering woman: depression that settles like November, pains that shoot from the neck and spine, rheumatic complaints that worsen with every period, and a heavy, gloomy, foreboding mind that fears madness. The dark, rheumatic, gloomy woman: that is Cimicifuga.

The substance, in plain words

Black cohosh is the tall white-candled herb of American forests. Its triterpene glycosides genuinely act on the neuro-endocrine system — it is a real, licensed menopause medicine in Europe and a top-selling botanical worldwide, with genuine trial evidence and a genuine liver-safety warning.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Gloomy, foreboding depression. The keynote of the mind: black, clouded, low — fear of death, of madness, of impending evil; worse during menses.
  • Fear of insanity. The books record the dread of losing the mind in the dark states.
  • Sighing and despondency. Sighs heavily, weeps readily, feels weighed down by life.

The body

  • Neck and spinal pains. The keynote: rheumatic, shooting pains from the occiput down the neck and spine, worse cold damp.
  • Worse during menses. The pains and the gloom climb with the cycle and ease after the flow.
  • Menopausal picture. Heat, heaviness, palpitation and mood together at the change — the books' classic menopause remedy.
  • Uterine and ovarian pains. Dull aching and bearing-down with the cycle, better after the flow.
  • Worse cold damp and motion. Wet weather and moving stiffen the spine further.
  • Better for warmth and eating. Warmth, food and the establishment of the flow bring relief.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Female organs and the cycle, neck and spine, mood and nerves, joints.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Native American women's herb — adopted by the Eclectics for rheumatism and the change of life — gave the proving its frame; the gloom-and-spine portrait became the remedy's identity in the classical library.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Black cohosh is one of the few women's botanicals with real clinical evidence: European trials support it for menopausal hot flushes, and it is a licensed medicine in Germany. Its rare liver-injury reports are genuine and debated — the plant is a real drug with a real pharmacovigilance file, not a folk rumour.
