Conium maculatum

Made from hemlock — the poison of Socrates, whose paralysing chemistry is real, and the classical remedy of hardening glands and vertigo.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a slow paralysis with hard glands: dizziness on every turn of the head, glands that grow hard and stony — breast, testes, neck — weakness that ascends, worse lying down, better closing the eyes and keeping still. The hard-gland, vertigo remedy of the state: that is Conium.

The substance, in plain words

Hemlock is the spotted umbellifer of Greek execution — the poison that killed Socrates, its coniine alkaloid paralysing from the feet upward with the mind clear to the end. The chemistry is real, documented for two thousand years; the proving gave the classical portrait of hard glands and turning vertigo.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Depression and lascivious melancholy. The books note a dark, hypochondriac gloom with lascivious fancies — the hemlock mind of the proving.
  • Anxiety about health. Fear of the hardening, of the growths, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction, with the vertiginous weakness.

The body

  • Vertigo on turning the head. The keynote: dizziness on every turn of the head or lying down — must keep the head still; closing the eyes helps.
  • Glands harden and grow stony. The keynote: breast, testes, neck — glands that turn hard and nodular; the books' great induration remedy.
  • Ascending weakness. Weakness climbing from the feet — the hemlock paralysis, mild, in the proving's mirror.
  • Worse lying down and turning. Lying down brings the vertigo and the weakness; rising slowly and stillness ease.
  • Worse sexual excess The books associate it with suppressed and disordered sexual life — the era's hypothesis.
  • Better for closing the eyes and stillness. Darkness behind closed lids and perfect stillness steady the world.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Glands (breast, testes, neck — hardening), balance and vertigo, nerves (ascending weakness), mind.

Where the classical picture comes from

Socrates' poison — the most famous execution in history — gave the proving its base: paralysis from below, mind clear, glands hardening. The remedy became the classical portrait of the stony gland and the turning vertigo, read by two millennia of poisoners and physicians alike.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Coniine is real, well-documented pharmacology: a nicotinic-receptor agonist that paralyses by depolarising the motor end-plate — the mode of Socrates' death, understood to the molecule today. No drug came of it; the plant is poison, pure and studied. The glands it portrayed are today's breast-clinic and testicular-examination territory — imaging, not hemlock.

Safety. A hard lump in breast or testis needs urgent medical assessment — examined, imaged, biopsied; vertigo with headache or weakness needs review.
