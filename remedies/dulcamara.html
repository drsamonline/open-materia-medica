Dulcamara

Made from bittersweet nightshade — the climbing Solanum of hedgerows whose glycoalkaloids guard it, and the classical remedy of ailments from damp.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of everything that follows getting wet: after sitting on damp ground, being caught in a rainstorm, or the first cold snap of a wet autumn — urticaria, facial eruptions, loose coughs, stiff backs and diarrhoea all arrive. One cause — cold damp — behind them all. Ailments from damp, worse wet weather: that is Dulcamara.

The substance, in plain words

Bittersweet nightshade is a woody hedgerow climber with scarlet berries, carrying the solanine-family glycoalkaloids that make all nightshades defended. The proving of the fresh stems and leaves gave the cold-damp constitutional portrait of the classical library.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Confusion and dullness. Slow, foggy thinking with the complaints; anxiety about health.
  • Low spirits Low spirits accompany the long wet-weather states.
  • Restlessness at night. Tossing with the wet-weather coughs and skin.

The body

  • Ailments from cold damp. The grand keynote: every complaint can be traced to damp — rain, wet feet, sitting on damp, river fog.
  • Urticaria and nettle-rash. Large white raised wheals, especially after chill; the books' damp-weather hive remedy.
  • Facial and scalp eruptions. Thick crusty eruptions of the scalp and face, worse winter.
  • Wet-weather cough. Loose, rattling, whooping-like coughs from every exposure to damp.
  • Stiffness and backache. Stiff neck and back from draught and damp — a small Rhus picture.
  • Diarrhoea of damp. Greenish, slimy summer diarrhoeas and autumn looseness after chilling.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Skin (urticarial, crusty), respiratory tract, back and muscles, bowels.

Where the classical picture comes from

The solanaceae proving gave the typical family dryness-and-irritation, sharpened by clinical experience into the damp-weather keynote: Dulcamara was the remedy of the marsh-dweller and the rain-soaked harvest.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Dulcamara's glycoalkaloids are genuine natural pesticides — the plant's own chemistry — and Solanum alkaloids are studied in pharmacology for their effects on the nervous system. The remedy's damp-weather territory in modern terms is seasonal allergy and weather-sensitive rheumatism, managed with antihistamines and conventional care.
