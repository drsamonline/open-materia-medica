Ferrum metallicum

Made from iron — the metal of blood itself, the world's most common deficiency, and the classical remedy of the falsely florid, easily exhausted circulation.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a paradoxical flush: a face that reddens easily and looks full of health, yet its owner is exhausted by the smallest effort, breathless on stairs, with ringing ears, pale mucous membranes betraying the flush, and a pulse that races then fails. False plethora: that is Ferrum.

The substance, in plain words

Iron is the metal at the centre of every red blood cell — the core of haemoglobin, without which blood cannot carry oxygen. Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional disorder on earth; iron supplementation is among the most effective, life-changing medicines ever deployed.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability from weakness. Cross, worse from contradiction, yet exhausted by the effort of anger.
  • Anxiety about health. Fear of illness, of the fluttering heart, worse in the quiet hours.
  • Melancholy. Gloomy, weepy temperament of the anaemic, better moving about gently.

The body

  • False plethora. The keynote: flushed, seemingly robust face with pale inner eyelids and easy flushing — 'full-blooded' looks hiding empty blood.
  • Prostration from least exertion. Walking, talking, even eating exhausts; must sit down mid-sentence.
  • Breathlessness and palpitation. The heart flutters with the least demand; ringing and roaring in the ears.
  • Pounding with least motion. The books describe the pulse bounding then collapsing after trifling effort.
  • Worse night and warmth. The flush and palpitation climb in warm rooms and the night hours.
  • Better for gentle walking. Slow, steady movement about the room — not exertion, just motion — relieves the oppression.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Blood and circulation, heart and pulse, respiration, female organs (flow).

Where the classical picture comes from

The proving of the metal gave the paradox of iron-starved strength — flush without blood, force without reserves. Clinically Ferrum attached to the chlorotic girls and the easily-winded of the era, whose 'false plethora' the old books describe precisely; modern readers will recognise iron-deficiency anaemia's exact face.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Iron is the single most important mineral in medicine: deficiency anaemia affects a quarter of the world's population, and iron supplementation — cheap, effective, proven — transforms fatigue, breathlessness and heart strain within weeks. The flushed-but-tired portrait on this page is a clinical photograph of a deficiency modern medicine can cure for pennies.
