Glonoinum

Made from nitroglycerin — the explosive chemistry that became angina's first medicine and dynamite's parent, and the classical remedy of the surging, throbbing, sunstruck head.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a head full of surging blood: throbbing, bursting, waves of blood beating against the skull — after the sun's heat, after the brandy, after the good news; the heart pounds in waves, the face flushes crimson, and every jolt of the carriage renews the storm. The surging-throbbing remedy: that is Glonoinum.

The substance, in plain words

Glonoinum is nitroglycerin — the same chemistry that Nobel tamed into dynamite and that medicine adopted as the first drug for angina in 1878. Its real pharmacology — releasing nitric oxide, relaxing vessels — won a Nobel Prize in 1998; the remedy's portrait is that chemistry, described by provers.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxious confusion. Bewildered, anxious, worse after the surging begins; cannot think for the throbbing.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise and jarring.
  • Laughter at serious things The proving notes odd, sudden mood-shifts with the vascular storm.

The body

  • Surging waves of blood to the head. The keynote: sensations of waves, throbs and surges of blood beating against the skull, in rhythm with the pulse.
  • After sun and heat. Sunstroke's headache, the brandy-flush, the hot-room throbbing — the books' great heat-headache remedy.
  • Worse jarring and shaking. Every jolt of the carriage or footstep renews the waves — the quoted modality.
  • Pounding heart in waves. The cardiac surging — palpitations that come and go in rhythmical waves.
  • Worse bending forward Stooping, shaking and heat bring the waves back.
  • Better for uncovering and stillness. Loosening the collar, opening the collar, lying still in cool air — the quoted relief.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

Nobel's explosive, reformulated as angina's first medicine in 1878, was proved the same decade: the surging, throbbing, wave-like portrait came straight from the chemistry — a remedy whose source and whose modern drug are the same molecule, in different dilutions of meaning.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Nitroglycerin is genuinely one of history's great drugs: under the tongue it relieves angina in minutes, its mechanism (nitric oxide signalling) won the 1998 Nobel Prize, and its explosive parentage built the modern world. Glonoinum's headache portrait — the vascular surge — is the drug's own pharmacology, felt; few remedies can claim their source molecule as a Nobel-winning medicine.
