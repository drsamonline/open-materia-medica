Hedera helix

Made from the ivy leaf — the climbing evergreen of every old wall, its leaf extract a genuine modern expectorant prescribed across Europe, and the classical remedy of the spasmodic cough.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a cough that racks the warm room: a spasmodic, racking cough worse in the warmth, the violent coughing fits of the old books, the sense of suffocation with the paroxysms, and the leaf of the wall-climber — a genuine modern cough medicine. The warm-room-cough remedy: that is Hedera helix.

The substance, in plain words

The ivy is the climbing evergreen of every old wall, its leaves the source of a genuinely modern expectorant: standardised ivy-leaf extract is a real, licensed cough medicine across Europe, its hederacoside saponins studied for loosening the bronchial spasm. The proving gave the leaf the spasmodic-cough portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety of the suffocative fits. The dread of the coughing paroxysm, worse in the warm room and at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the racking, breathless fits.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, recurrent states.

The body

  • Spasmodic cough worse in warmth. The keynote: the racking, spasmodic cough worse in the warmth of the room — the books' great warm-room cough remedy.
  • Violent coughing fits. The exhausting, breath-stealing paroxysms of the books' description.
  • Sense of suffocation. The suffocative sensation riding the fits.
  • Sticky, difficult mucus. The books add the tough, hard-to-raise mucus the ivy loosens.
  • Worse warmth and night. The warm room and the night own the cough.
  • Better for cool open air. Cool, fresh air eases the paroxysms.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

warm rooms; the warmth of the bed; night; damp cold.cool open air; sitting up; rest.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Bronchi and lungs, respiratory mucosa, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The wall-climber's proving gave the warm-room cough; Hedera became the classical remedy of the spasmodic, suffocative cough of the warm hours — the leaf of every old wall serving the chest it shaded.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Ivy-leaf extract is genuinely a modern cough medicine: standardised preparations are licensed and prescribed across Europe for productive cough, with clinical evidence for loosening bronchial spasm — the wall-climber's saponins are a real, current remedy. The spasmodic coughs it portrayed are now managed with modern bronchodilators and antitussives; the leaf's career is real and ongoing.
