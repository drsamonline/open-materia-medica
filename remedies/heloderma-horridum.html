Heloderma horridum

Made from the venom of the Gila monster — the slow lizard of the American deserts whose chemistry gave modern medicine a genuinely revolutionary diabetes drug.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a cold, bluish, convulsive state: coldness and cyanosis of the parts, the slow pulse and the stupor, the tendency to convulsions of the proving, and the books' great lizard remedy — behind which sits one of the great pharmacology stories of the century. The cold-and-slow remedy: that is Heloderma.

The substance, in plain words

The Gila monster is the heavy, bead-skinned lizard of the American deserts, slow-moving and venomous — its venom carrying exendin-4, a peptide genuinely studied by endocrinologists and the direct ancestor of exenatide, the first modern diabetes drug of the incretin class. The proving gave the lizard the cold-and-slow portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Dullness and stupor. The keynote of the mind: the dull, drowsy, slow mental state — the lizard's pace in the portrait.
  • Anxiety and fear. Fear of death, worse at night, under the dullness.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from contradiction and the cold.

The body

  • Coldness and cyanosis. The keynote: coldness and blueness of the parts, the slow, cold circulation — the Gila monster's desert physiology in the proving.
  • Slow pulse and stupor. The slow pulse with the drowsy, stupid state of the books.
  • Convulsive tendency. The books add the tendency to convulsions and the spasmodic states.
  • Worse cold and damp. Cold and wet aggravate the cold, slow state.
  • Better for warmth. Warmth and rest ease the coldness and the slowness.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Circulation and temperature, nervous system, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The desert lizard's proving gave the cold-and-slow portrait; Heloderma became the classical remedy of the cold, cyanotic, sluggish state — and its venom's peptide chemistry waited a century to give medicine one of its great modern drugs.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Exendin-4 is genuinely one of the great pharmacology stories of the century: the Gila monster's venom peptide, studied because the lizard eats rarely yet keeps its blood sugar steady, became the template for exenatide — the first incretin drug for type 2 diabetes, transforming treatment worldwide and founding a whole drug class. The slow lizard of the desert holds a genuine revolution in its venom: few sources in this library can say their molecule changed a disease's treatment so directly.

Safety. Cold, blue extremities, a slow pulse or drowsiness need urgent assessment; any suspected venomous bite is an emergency.
