Hepar sulphuris

Made from oyster shells heated with sulphur — Hahnemann's own 'calcined oyster' preparation; the classical remedy of the touch-me-not abscess.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the most hypersensitive patient in the materia medica: the slightest touch, the lightest draft, a splinter-like pain in every sore, anger and impatience when in pain, and abscesses that ripen fast with throbbing, stitching pain — better for warmth and damp, worse for cold dry wind. Touch-intolerance, splinter-pains, fast suppuration: that is Hepar sulphuris.

The substance, in plain words

Hahnemann made Hepar by heating oyster-shell lime with sulphur until fused — a crude calcium sulphide. The chemistry is simple (a sulphide of calcium), but the clinical portrait it earned in the old books is one of the most distinct in the whole library.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Irritability from pain. Impulse to anger, quarrelling, complaining when ill; the books note childish peevishness.
  • Hypersensitivity. Oversensitive to pain, to touch, to draughts, to people — 'the touch-me-not'.
  • Fear and anger mixed. Fear of knives and wind; anger at trifles; weeping from pain.

The body

  • Cannot bear touch. The keynote: dressings, examinations, even air on the part cause distress; wants it wrapped warmly.
  • Splinter-like pains. Sharp, sticking, splinter sensations in throat, ulcers, skin lesions.
  • Abscesses that ripen quickly. Every cut threatens pus; boils and felons throb and swell; the classical early-suppuration remedy.
  • Throat picture. Quinsy-like throats, sensation of a plug or fishbone, worse swallowing, better hot drinks.
  • Chilliness. Intensely chilly, worse from the slightest cold dry wind; wraps up head and throat.
  • Skin eruptions. Eczema cracked and oozing, worse winter and wind; old books use it for croupy coughs with loose rattling.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Skin and glands (abscesses, ulcers), throat and tonsils, respiratory mucosa, nervous sensitivity to pain.

Where the classical picture comes from

The fused lime-sulphur preparation proved with the classic sulphur picture sharpened: heat, sensitivity, suppuration. Clinicians of the era used it wherever boils and throats ran to pus quickly, and its reputation was built in the suppurating diseases — tonsillitis, felons, skin sepsis — that filled pre-antibiotic consulting rooms.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Calcium sulphide chemistry is straightforward, and sulphur itself keeps a real, modest place in dermatology as an antiparasitic and keratolytic. But the conditions Hepar classically addressed — quinsy, boils, felons — are exactly the infections that antibiotics transformed; today a rapidly worsening abscess or peritonsillar swelling is a prompt for medical care, not patience.

Safety. A painful swelling of the throat with drooling or difficulty breathing, or a spreading skin infection with fever, are emergencies.
