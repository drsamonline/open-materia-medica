Hydrastis canadensis

Made from goldenseal — the yellow-rooted North American herb whose berberine and hydrastine are real alkaloids, and the classical tonic of torpid membranes.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of membranes that have stopped working: thick, yellow, ropy mucus hanging in strings from nose or throat, a weak stomach that ulcerates and will not heal, a torpid liver, and a general 'won't-come-right' debility in pale, flabby people. Ropy yellow catarrh, torpid ulceration: that is Hydrastis.

The substance, in plain words

Goldenseal is the golden-rooted herb of North American forests, rich in berberine and hydrastine — genuine isoquinoline alkaloids. It was the Eclectic school's great tonic, used for ulcers, catarrhs and debility; its chemistry is active enough that modern medicine respects it and folk overuse depletes the wild plant.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Low spirits and foreboding. Gloomy, anxious about health, worse in damp weather; the books paint the discouraged chronic invalid.
  • Irritability. Cross over trifles with the constant catarrhal drag.
  • Dullness. Slow thinking with the general torpidity.

The body

  • Ropy yellow mucus. The keynote: thick, yellow, stringy mucus — from nose, throat, stomach, vagina — clinging in ropes.
  • Ulcerated, torpid surfaces. Weak-healing ulcers of the stomach and bowel; the books' great tonic of chronic mucous membrane.
  • Weak digestion. Sinking at the stomach, poor appetite, everything lies heavy; liver torpid.
  • Constipation of inactivity. No urge, no power; the rectum feels paralysed rather than bound.
  • Thin, flabby build. Pale, exhausted, easily depleted; cachectic tendency in the old books.
  • Worse damp and cold. Catarrhs and pains worsen in wet weather.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

damp cold weather; suppressed discharges; rich food; night air.rest; warmth; open air in dry weather.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Mucous membranes (stomach, nose, bowel, vagina), liver, skin (sallow), general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Eclectic physicians used goldenseal for exactly the states the proving confirmed — ropy catarrh, weak ulceration, torpid digestion. The remedy became the chronic-tonic portrait of the library, applied wherever membranes had gone quiet.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Berberine — goldenseal's golden alkaloid — is one of the best-documented botanical actives: antimicrobial, studied in gut infection, diabetes and cholesterol. Hydrastine was an official astringent-uterotonic of the old pharmacopoeia. Goldenseal is a real alkaloid pharmacy, and its wild overharvesting is a modern conservation concern.
