Justicia adhatoda

Made from the Malabar nut tree — the Ayurvedic vasaka whose alkaloid vasicine gave modern medicine the cough drug ambroxol.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a chest cold that begins with sneezing and a tight, dry cough, then turns loose with rattling mucus, worse in the evening and night, with a raw, sore chest and exhaustion from coughing. Sneezing onset, tight-then-loose cough: that is Justicia adhatoda.

The substance, in plain words

Vasaka, the Malabar nut, is one of Ayurveda's foremost respiratory herbs. Its chief alkaloid, vasicine, was chemically transformed by twentieth-century pharmacology into bromhexine and then ambroxol — modern mucolytics prescribed worldwide for chest congestion. Few classical remedies can claim so direct a descendant in real medicine.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Dullness with the cold. Heavy head, indifference, desire to lie still during the acute phase.
  • Irritability at night. Worse mood when the cough keeps the household awake.
  • Anxiety about breathing. Fearful sensation of constriction during the tight early stage.

The body

  • Sneezing onset. The cold begins in a burst of sneezing with watery eyes — a signature of the remedy.
  • Tight then loose cough. A dry, tight, exhausting cough that gradually loosens into rattling, difficult mucus.
  • Worse evening and night. The cough climbs from evening onwards and peaks in the night hours.
  • Raw sore chest. Pain and rawness behind the sternum, worse from coughing and pressure.
  • Thirstlessness to thirst. Like many respiratory remedies, little thirst in the early dry stage.
  • Hoarseness and larynx. Voice husky; larynx raw; the books use it across the whole bronchitic picture.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Bronchi and respiratory tract, larynx, chest walls.

Where the classical picture comes from

Vasaka's centuries of Ayurvedic use for cough and chest congestion preceded the homeopathic proving; Hahnemann's school adopted a plant whose respiratory reputation was already ancient — and whose alkaloid would later become a modern drug.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Vasicine is the genuine parent molecule of bromhexine and ambroxol — standard mucolytics that thin chest mucus and are among the world's most prescribed respiratory medicines. The Malabar nut thus holds a rare distinction in this library: a classical remedy whose chemistry became literal textbook pharmacology.
