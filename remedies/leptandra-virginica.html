Leptandra virginica

Made from black root — the American prairie herb whose extract, leptandrin, held an official place in the old pharmacopoeias as a liver laxative.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a liver-bound bowel: a torpid liver with a black, tarry, sticky stool, the tongue coated dark, the right side dull and heavy, and a dragging malaise that clears when the bowels finally move. Black stools, hepatic torpor, relief on evacuation: that is Leptandra.

The substance, in plain words

Black root is the prairie Veronicastrum whose dried root was exported as a cholagogue — leptandrin sat in the official dispensatories as a liver-purgative of the nineteenth century. A real botanical drug, since retired; its proving gave the hepatic-torpor portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Dullness and languor. Heavy-minded, indifferent, worse in the morning with the coated tongue.
  • Irritability. Crossness from the dragging right-sided discomfort.
  • Despondency. The books pair it with the low spirits of the chronically bilious.

The body

  • Black, tarry stool. The keynote: dark, tarry, adhesive stool of the hepatic state — not bloody, but black as if burned.
  • Hepatic torpor. Liver dull, heavy, congested; right hypochondrium oppressive.
  • Dark-coated tongue. The tongue coated dark-brown or black along the centre.
  • Relief from evacuation. The whole state — head, mood, liver — lightens after the bowels move.
  • Bilious vomiting. Vomiting of bile with the constipation-diarrhea alternation.
  • Worse morning. The heaviness and coating are worst on waking.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Liver and bile ducts, bowels (black stool), stomach, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

Leptandrin's official career gave the proving its frame — a liver-directed laxative produced exactly the hepatic-torpor picture the remedy was then used for; a tidy circle of drug, poison and portrait.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Black root was a genuine, official drug — an American contribution to the pharmacopoeias, retired when safer cholagogues and, later, liver science arrived. Its historical role is real even if its chemistry now belongs to pharmaceutical history.
