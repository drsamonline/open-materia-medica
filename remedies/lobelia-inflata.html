Lobelia inflata

Made from Indian tobacco — the American herb whose lobeline mimics nicotine, was studied for smoking cessation, and became the classical asthma-with-nausea remedy.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of asthma married to nausea: a sensation of oppression at the chest, breathlessness with faintness, cold sweat, nausea rising with every paroxysm, and vomiting that ends the attack — worse in cold air, with a weak stomach behind it all. Asthma with nausea, attack ending in vomit: that is Lobelia.

The substance, in plain words

Lobelia is the American 'Indian tobacco', its lobeline an alkaloid that mimics and then blocks nicotine's actions — studied in the twentieth century as a smoking-cessation drug. In overdose it vomits and sweats; in the proving it gave the chest-and-stomach portrait of the classical books.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety of the dyspnea. Fear, dread of suffocation with the oppression; worse alone at night.
  • Irritability. Cross, worried, worse after the attack's exhaustion.
  • Weak-mindedness. The books note a feeble, anxious mind with the feeble chest.

The body

  • Asthma with nausea. The keynote: oppression of the chest with nausea — the two systems protest together.
  • Attack ending in vomiting. The paroxysm breaks in vomiting and cold sweat; then relief.
  • Faintness and sweat. Pallor, cold sweat, weak pulse with the breathlessness.
  • Worse cold air. Cold damp air and cold water on the chest aggravate.
  • Weak stomach. Gastric weakness behind the chest — the books pair Lobelia with dyspeptic asthma.
  • Pram The oppression and nausea are worst in the morning hours.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

Lobelia's overdose pharmacology — nausea, vomiting, sweat, oppression — is the proving; clinically the remedy attached to the dyspeptic asthmatic whose attacks ended at the basin, a figure well known to the old practitioners.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Lobeline's nicotine-mimicry made it a genuine candidate drug for smoking cessation in the twentieth century — a real pharmacological lead, since abandoned for better agents. Lobelia remains a working example of an autonomic-active plant, its chemistry authentically on the nicotinic pathway.

Safety. Asthma attacks with faintness, blue lips or inability to speak need emergency care; severe breathlessness is never to be managed at home.
