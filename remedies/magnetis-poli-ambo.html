Magnetis poli ambo

Made from the whole magnet — sugar of milk exposed to the entire mass; the classical portrait of the magnet's pull: burning lancinations, broken-joint pains and a nail-like headache.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a body under a magnet: burning lancinations everywhere, joints that ache as if the bones were broken where their cartilages meet, shooting and jerking, a headache as if a nail were driven in — and old wounds that start to bleed afresh. The magnet's strange pull on living tissue: that is Magnetis poli ambo.

The substance, in plain words

The whole magnet, north and south pole together: sugar of milk or distilled water is exposed to the influence of the entire magnet and then potentised. Hahnemann tested the magnet and each pole on himself and his provers; the symptoms of the whole magnet were gathered from powerful horse-shoe magnets held in the hands for an hour at a time.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Busy precipitation. A hurried, driven feeling; absence of mind.
  • Irresolution. Inadvertence and hesitation, with a tendency to anger and rage.
  • Action and reaction. Hahnemann noted that each pole, applied twice or oftener, produces alternating actions resembling those of the opposite pole.

The body

  • Burning lancinations throughout the body, with pains as if the joints were broken where two cartilages touch, and shooting and jerkings.
  • Headache as if a nail were driven into the head, especially after a fit of anger; a digging, stupefying headache relieved by passing wind.
  • Teeth. Toothache worse from cold and from contact with anything cold.
  • Old wounds tend to bleed afresh; prolapse of the anus; inflammation of the testicle; indolent ulcers.
  • Sleep disturbed, with the bruised feeling worst on waking.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

The nerves and muscles, the joints and bones, the blood, and old wounds.

Where the classical picture comes from

This is Hahnemann's own pathogenesis. He tested the whole magnet and each pole separately, holding a horse-shoe magnet capable of lifting twelve pounds in his hands for an hour at a time, and described the alternating actions of the poles. Clarke adds the cases of Andry, Thouret and Unzer, and the popular nineteenth-century use of magnetic rings for rheumatism.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Magnetism is genuinely woven through modern medicine: MRI builds its images from nuclear magnetic resonance in a strong field, and transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) now treats depression by stimulating the cortex with magnetic pulses. Static 'magnet therapy' for pain is widely sold, but the evidence for it is weak and inconsistent; the magnetic fields that change biology are the strong, pulsing ones, not a bracelet.
