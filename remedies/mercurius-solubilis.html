Mercurius solubilis

Made from mercury — the quicksilver of alchemists, the poison of hatters and mirror-makers, and the classical remedy of everything offensive and sweaty.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a damp, sweating, trembling person whose every secretion is offensive — metallic-tasting saliva dripping at night, sour sweat that does not relieve, bloody offensive stool and urine — with spongy, bleeding gums, loose teeth, ulcerated mouth and trembling hands. Night-sweats, salivation, tremor and offensiveness: that is Mercurius.

The substance, in plain words

Mercury is the only metal liquid at room temperature, prized since antiquity and feared since the madness of hatters. It poisons nerves and kidneys, loosens teeth and blackens gums — and its compounds, however toxic, were for centuries the only treatment for syphilis. Few poisons have been so long a medicine.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Restless irresolution. Weak memory, hesitation, loss of will; the books describe a 'broken-down' mental state.
  • Suspicious melancholy. Fear of being poisoned or robbed; peevishness in illness.
  • Hurry and trembling thought. Slowed speech but inner restlessness; sweating with anxiety.

The body

  • Offensive everything. Saliva, sweat, breath, stool and urine all carry a peculiar offensiveness — the remedy's trademark.
  • Night-sweats without relief. Profuse sweat at night that brings no comfort, drenching and sour.
  • Mouth and gums. Spongy, bleeding, ulcerated gums; loosening teeth; metallic taste; the classic mercurial mouth.
  • Throat and glands. Raw throat, ulcerated tonsils, swollen glands — the books use it in every suppurating throat.
  • Stool and urine. Slimy, bloody, offensive stool with tenesmus; burning urine; the classical dysentery picture.
  • Tremor and bone pains. Fine trembling of hands and tongue; nightly bone pains, worse in damp weather.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Mouth, gums and salivary glands, throat and lymphatics, skin and mucous membranes, kidneys and nerves.

Where the classical picture comes from

Chronic mercury poisoning — the trembling 'mad hatter', the salivating, black-toothed gilder, the ulcerated mouth of the felt-worker — was among the best-described occupational diseases of the nineteenth century. The proving merely confirmed what the workshops taught: mercury makes everything sweat, salivate and stink.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Mercury's compounds were the standard, desperate treatment for syphilis for four centuries; mercury preservatives (thiomersal) served vaccines until safer alternatives arrived; mercury thermometers taught a generation of nurses. Its clinical legacy today is almost entirely toxicological — mercury is a famous environmental poison, and its poisoning syndrome remains textbook medicine. The crude metal has no therapeutic place in modern practice.
