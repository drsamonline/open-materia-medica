Murex purpurea

Made from the purple murex — the sea snail that dyed the ancient world Tyrian purple, and the classical remedy of intense female excitement.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a pelvis in overdrive: intense, driving sexual excitement with the least touch unbearable, a uterus that feels it must bear down, sensations that run from the nipples to the womb, sleeplessness from the inner fire — and relief, strangely, from hard pressure and after sleep. The over-excited, touch-intolerant woman: that is Murex.

The substance, in plain words

The purple murex is the sea snail of the eastern Mediterranean — thousands of snails to dye one robe of Tyrian purple, the colour of emperors. Its organic chemistry of bromine-related compounds was genuinely believed, in the era, to explain its effect on the sexual organs; the proving gave the over-excitement portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Restless over-excitement. The keynote of the mind: driven, sleepless, inwardly aflame; sadness alternating with excitement.
  • Anxiety and sadness. Weeping, melancholy, worse in the quiet hours, after the excitement spends itself.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from the least touch or approach.

The body

  • Intense sexual excitement. The keynote: heightened sensation with the least touch, driving excitement of the female organs.
  • Uterine bearing-down. As if the uterus must press down and out; must sit with legs crossed.
  • Nipple-to-womb sensations. The books describe sensations running from the breasts to the womb — the remedy's distinctive path.
  • Worse from least touch. Even clothing or the touch of the bed-clothes aggravates.
  • Better from pressure and after sleep. Firm pressure on the pelvis, and sleep itself, relieve — quoted in the books.
  • Menses irregular and painful. The cycle arrives painful, irregular, with the whole picture in flower.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Female organs and sexual excitement, uterus (bearing-down), breasts, mood.

Where the classical picture comes from

The purple snail's dye fame was imperial; its proving gave the remedy the over-excitement-bearing-down portrait. Clinically Murex attached to the touch-intolerant, over-driven female state of the old books.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Tyrian purple is the murex's real empire: the dye chemistry of the ancient world — thousands of snails per robe — and its bromine-bearing precursors were genuine organic chemistry, once theorised (plausibly, incorrectly) to act on the sexual organs. The snail's pharmacology is now dye-history; the states it portrayed are today endocrinology, gynaecology and sexual-health medicine's territory.

Safety. Persistent over-excitement with sleeplessness, or any pelvic pain with bleeding, deserve medical review; distress of the mind deserves care.
