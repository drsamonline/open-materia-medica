Pinus sylvestris

Made from the Scots pine — the Caledonian forest's great tree, its turpentine chemistry genuinely expectorant, and the classical remedy of the chronic bronchitis.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a cough that owns the winter: the chronic bronchitis of the books, the cough with its copious expectoration, the winter chest of the damp-worse constitution, and the great pine of the Caledonian forest. The winter-cough pine: that is Pinus sylvestris.

The substance, in plain words

The Scots pine is the great tree of the Caledonian forest, its resin the source of the turpentines — genuinely expectorant and antiseptic chemistry of the old chest shelf, used across the dispensaries for the chronic cough. The proving gave the pine the winter-chest portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about health. Worry over the chronic cough, the winter, the breathing, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant, dragging cough.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, winter-returning states.

The body

  • Chronic bronchitis. The keynote: the chronic, winter-returning bronchitis of the books — the pine's great chapter.
  • Cough with copious expectoration. The cough with the copious, heavy expectoration of the same chapter.
  • Winter chest of the damp-worse. The damp-worse, winter-owning chest of the proving.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night thicken the chest.
  • Better for open air and the resin's memory. Open air and the turpentine's old clearing ease the pine state.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Bronchi and lungs, respiratory mucosa, general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Caledonian pine's proving gave the winter-cough portrait; Pinus sylvestris became the classical remedy of the chronic bronchitis — the great forest tree serving the chest its resin cleared.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

The pine's real chemistry is the turpentine family — genuinely expectorant and antiseptic, the old chest shelf's real resins, documented across the dispensaries. The chronic bronchitis it portrayed is now respiratory medicine's — inhalers and smoking cessation give real answers; the tree's chemistry is honest, its territory transformed.
