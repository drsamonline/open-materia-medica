Plantago major

Made from the plantain leaf — the wayside wound herb of every cottage garden, and the classical remedy of the toothache, the ear and the tobacco aversion.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a toothache that reaches the ear: decayed teeth aching and throbbing into the ear, with restlessness, aversion to tobacco — the mere thought of smoking brings nausea — and the books' companion note of bed-wetting. The tooth-to-ear remedy: that is Plantago.

The substance, in plain words

The plantain is the broad-leaved wayside weed of the world's paths, its leaves a genuine folk wound-herb — chewed for stings, poulticed for wounds — with documented soothing and antimicrobial chemistry. The proving gave the remedy its tooth-to-ear portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Aversion to tobacco. The keynote of the mind: the very thought of tobacco brings disgust and nausea — the books' celebrated keynote.
  • Restlessness with the pain. Tossing, unable to settle with the tooth and ear ache.
  • Anxiety and irritability. Cross, worried, worse at night with the pain.

The body

  • Toothache into the ear. The keynote: decayed, aching teeth with the pain shooting into the ear — the tooth-to-ear path of the books.
  • Restlessness and sleeplessness with it. The pain drives the sufferer from the bed, worse at night.
  • Enuresis of the books. The companion note — bed-wetting, especially with the tobacco aversion.
  • Earache and noises. The ear shares the tooth's complaint, with noises and sensitiveness.
  • Worse cold air and night. Cold air on the teeth and the night hours aggravate.
  • Better for pressure and warmth. Pressure on the cheek and warmth ease the ache.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Teeth and gums, ears, bladder (bed-wetting), mouth.

Where the classical picture comes from

The wayside herb's proving gave the tooth-to-ear portrait with its strange tobacco keynote; the old books used Plantago for the decayed-tooth sufferer whose ear shared the ache and whose very thought of tobacco turned the stomach.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Plantain is genuinely one of folk medicine's most respectable wound-herbs: its leaves contain soothing mucilage and antimicrobial compounds, and it remains a standard of herbal first-aid in many traditions — chewed on stings, poulticed on wounds. The toothache it portrayed belongs to the dentist; the herb's demulcent chemistry is real and modest.
