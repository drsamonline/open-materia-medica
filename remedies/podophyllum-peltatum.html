Podophyllum peltatum

Made from the mayapple — the American woodland plant whose podophyllotoxin treats genital warts and gave birth to the chemotherapy drug etoposide.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of morning gushing: profuse, hot, yellow-green, offensive diarrhea that drives the person out of bed at dawn — painless yet exhausting, often with prolapse of the rectum — worse after eating and drinking, better lying flat on the stomach. Dawn diarrhea, painless and gushing: that is Podophyllum.

The substance, in plain words

The mayapple is the umbrella-leaved plant of American woods; its rhizome carries podophyllotoxin, a genuine modern drug — applied as the standard treatment for genital warts — and the parent molecule of etoposide, a front-line chemotherapy drug for lung and testicular cancers.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Indifference with the suffering. Dull, apathetic, low-spirited during the long morning evacuations.
  • Anxiety about health. Fear of disease, worse in the damp morning hours.
  • Delirium in fever. The books note muttering delirium and picking at the bedclothes in the hot states.

The body

  • Dawn gushing diarrhea. The keynote: profuse, painless, hot, offensive stool gushing out on waking, worse around 4 a.m.
  • Prolapse and bearing down. Rectum prolapses with the stool; the whole pelvic floor seems to give way.
  • Worse eating and drinking. Anything by mouth sets the bowel off again; thirst with vomiting of the drink.
  • Liver region. Pain and congestion of the right side, radiating to the back; jaundice tendency.
  • Green, frothy stools. Yellow-green, frothy, offensive, sometimes alternating with constipation.
  • Better lying on the abdomen. Pressure of the bed on the belly eases the bearing-down — the quoted modality.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Liver and bowel, rectum (prolapse), pelvic floor, digestion.

Where the classical picture comes from

The fresh rhizome is a violent purgative — the proving recorded the gushing dawn stool and prolapse; clinically the remedy served the dysenteries and summer diarrhoeas of the American frontier, where the mayapple grew and the complaint was constant.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Podophyllum's chemistry is one of the library's modern triumphs: podophyllotoxin is the standard topical cure for genital warts, and the semi-synthetic etoposide built from its skeleton remains a chemotherapy mainstay. The woodland plant became oncology — a direct, undeniable line from source to drug.
