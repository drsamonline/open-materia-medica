Sabal serrulata

Made from saw palmetto — the American palm berry that became the most-studied botanical for the prostate, and the classical remedy of prostatic enlargement.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of the older man's slow stream: urination that starts late, dribbles, stops and starts, with a constant sense of fullness in the prostate region, sexual disturbance, and the whole slow tyranny of the enlarging gland. The prostatic remedy: that is Sabal.

The substance, in plain words

Saw palmetto is the dwarf palm of the American southeast, its berries rich in fatty-acid and sterol chemistry. It is the most-studied botanical for benign prostatic enlargement — real randomised trials, real meta-analyses, real (if debated) benefit — and the proving gave the classical prostatic portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and irritability. Cross, worried, worse in damp weather; the books paint the depressed prostatic temperament.
  • Fear of disease. Fixed worry over the gland, the stream, the manhood.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, progressive states.

The body

  • Prostatic enlargement. The keynote: the enlarged prostate — slow starting, weak stream, dribbling, residual fullness.
  • Sense of fullness in the gland. A constant sensation of weight and fullness in the prostatic region.
  • Sexual disturbance. The books pair the urinary with the sexual — diminished, disturbed, painful.
  • Worse cold damp. Wet weather thickens the symptoms; cold chills aggravate.
  • Chronic prostatic catarrh. Discharges of the chronic prostatic type, milky, with the urinary complaints.
  • Better for warmth and dry weather. Warmth and the dry season ease the stream.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

The palm berry's folk use for 'old men's disease' among the American south gave the proving its frame; the remedy became the classical portrait of prostatic enlargement, written before the gland had a treatment worth the name.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Saw palmetto is the most-investigated prostate botanical in the world: meta-analyses of randomised trials show modest but real improvement in urinary symptoms of benign prostatic enlargement, and it sits in the pharmacy of most countries. The honest modern note: for significant obstruction, modern medicine's alpha-blockers and surgery work better — the berry is for the mild case, the scalpel for the severe.
