Sanicula

The classical remedy prepared from the Sanicula spring water — the mineral water of the Michigan health resort, and the classical remedy of the sweating head and the foul stool.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a head that sweats and a stool that stinks: the drenching sweat of the scalp of the books, the foul, sour stool, the chronic eczema and the warty eruptions, and the mineral water of the old Michigan resort. The spring-water remedy: that is Sanicula.

The substance, in plain words

Sanicula is prepared from the mineral water of the Sanicula spring of the old Michigan health resort — a genuine spa water, bottled and prescribed in the era of the health resorts, its mineral chemistry the salt-sulfur blend of the springs. The proving gave the spring water the sweat-and-stool portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about health. Worry over the sweat, the stool, the chronic skin, worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant discomfort.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic states.

The body

  • Drenching sweat of the scalp. The keynote: the profuse, drenching sweat of the head of the books.
  • Foul, sour stool. The foul, sour, offensive evacuations of the same chapter.
  • Chronic eczema and warts. The books add the chronic eczema and the wart-like eruptions.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night thicken the sweat and the skin.
  • Better for dry weather and the spring's memory. Dry weather and rest ease the spring-water state.

Modalities (worse and better factors)

Worse from ⬇Better from ⬇
cold damp weather; night; rich food.dry weather; rest; the spring's course.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Sweat glands (scalp), bowels (foul stool), skin (eczema, warts), general vitality.

Where the classical picture comes from

The Michigan spring's proving gave the sweat-and-stool portrait; Sanicula became the classical remedy of the drenching head-sweat and the foul stool — the resort's mineral water serving the constitutions that visited it.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Sanicula's real story is the American health-resort era — the mineral springs bottled and prescribed in the spa century, their salt-sulfur chemistry genuine and their efficacy the honest placebo-and-mineral blend of the time. The sweating head and foul stool it portrayed are now paediatric and gastroenterological assessment; the spring belongs to the history of the resorts.
