Spigelia anthelmia

Made from pinkroot — the American worm-flower whose genuine anthelmintic chemistry made it official, and the classical remedy of the left-sided, stitching, heart-pained head.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a left-sided agony in waves: stitching, tearing pains over the left eye and orbit, worsening with every beat of the pulse, spreading from the head to the neck and arm, worse for motion, touch and noise, in a person of nervous temperament. The left-sided stitching head: that is Spigelia.

The substance, in plain words

Pinkroot is the American worm-flower — its spigeline and related chemistry genuinely expels worms, and it was an official anthelmintic of the old United States Pharmacopeia, used for generations. The proving gave the remedy its famous left-sided neuralgic portrait, read in every old headache chapter.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety and nervousness. Fearful, worse in the quiet hours; the books paint the nervous, neuralgic temperament.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise, touch and contradiction.
  • Despondency. Gloomy during the long recurring attacks.

The body

  • Left-sided stitching head-pain. The keynote: violent stitching, tearing pains beginning over the left eye, boring into the orbit and temple.
  • Worse with the pulse's beat. The pain worsens with every heartbeat — the books' celebrated observation.
  • Worse motion, touch and noise. The least movement, touch, stooping or jarring renews the tearing; the sufferer lies still in the dark.
  • Radiation to neck and arm. The pain spreads along the left side into neck, shoulder and arm.
  • Worse morning The attacks return by their own clock, often with the day's beginning.
  • Better for rest and quiet. Perfect stillness, darkness and lying on the painless side bring ease.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Head and left orbit (neuralgia), nerves, heart-region association, eyes.

Where the classical picture comes from

The official worm-flower's proving gave the library's most precisely-located head-pain: left eye, stitching, pulse-synchronous. Spigelia became the classical remedy of the violent left-sided neuralgia, read beside its worm-expelling chemistry — a flower with two careers, both real.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Pinkroot's anthelmintic chemistry was genuinely effective — an official worm remedy of the USP for decades, retired when safer vermifuges (santonin's successors, then the modern benzimidazoles) arrived. Its overdose toxicology is also documented. The neuralgias it portrayed are today neurology's — trigeminal neuralgia managed with modern anticonvulsants and microsurgery.
