Sulphur

Made from elemental brimstone — burned in fumigation since antiquity, the active agent in sulphur ointment, and the great 'antipsoric' of the classical world.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of heat that must escape: burning feet shoved out of the covers at night, a hot scalp, redness and itching of every orifice, skin that burns and itches and must be scratched to bleeding — yet the person hates water, stands worse, feels worse at 11 a.m., and is famously averse to bathing. Hot, red, itchy, dirty-feeling and thirsty for the open air: that is Sulphur.

The substance, in plain words

Sulphur is the pale yellow element of volcanoes and matches, known since antiquity as brimstone. Burned, it fumigates; applied, it kills mites and fungi. As a homeopathic remedy it is the 'antipsoric' Hahnemann set at the head of chronic disease — and its real actions as an antiparasitic and keratolytic are the oldest genuine pharmacology in the entire materia medica.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • The philosopher untidy. The classical type is abstracted, theorising, indifferent to dress and order — 'ragged philosopher'.
  • Irritable and idle by turns. Alternating indolence and bursts of purposeless activity.
  • Selfishness in illness. The books describe a childish selfish streak when sick, with dislike of interference.

The body

  • Burning heat with redness. Heat of head, palms, soles; burning of ears, anus and skin; soles burn so they are thrust out of bed.
  • Itching, burning skin. Itching that burns and is scratched until raw; worse night, heat and washing; often dry, scaly, unhealthy skin.
  • Red orifices. A red rim at the anus, lips, eyelids or nostrils is a classical confirming sign.
  • Aversion to bathing. Worse from washing — one of the most quoted peculiarities in the old books.
  • Morning aggravation. Feels worse around 11 a.m.; also worse from standing, which brings heat to the head.
  • Thirst and appetite. Craves sweets and alcohol; hungry at 11 a.m.; thirst for cold drinks.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Skin, circulation and heat regulation, mucous membranes, digestion and liver.

Where the classical picture comes from

Sulphur fumigation and ointment were the standard treatment for scabies and skin disease for two thousand years before Hahnemann made it a constitutional remedy; the proving added the heat, redness and itching portrait. Its classical fame as the 'antipsoric' rests on real antiparasitic chemistry.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Sulphur ointment remains in modern pharmacopoeias as a safe, effective treatment for scabies in infants and pregnant women where permethrin is unsuitable, and sulphur-based preparations are used as keratolytics and antifungals. It is the one classical remedy whose crude substance never left real medicine.
