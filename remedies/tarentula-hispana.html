Tarentula hispana

Made from the Spanish wolf spider — the Lycosa of the legendary tarantella dance, and the classical remedy of rhythm and relentless motion.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a body that must keep moving: ceaseless restless activity, dancing and rocking to relieve the inner torment, destructiveness, tearing and throwing, with extreme sensitivity — worse from touch, from seeing others touched, from noise. Restless rhythm, music-loving, destructive energy: that is Tarentula.

The substance, in plain words

The wolf spider of southern Europe is the creature of the tarantella legend — the dance that supposedly cured the spider's bite. Its venom is a neurotoxin studied for its effects on ion channels; the dance of the proving — ceaseless motion — mirrors both the venom's agitation and its folk cure.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Ceaseless restless activity. The keynote: must move, dance, rock, drum — stillness is torment; energy with no direction.
  • Destructiveness. Tearing clothes and bedding, breaking objects, spiteful mischief; then sudden remorse.
  • Extreme sensitivity. Worse from seeing others touched or in pain, from noise, from music played badly; startles violently.
  • Cunning and mockery. Mimics, teases, plays tricks — the books paint an agile, mocking, theatrical temperament.

The body

  • Better for music and dancing. The famous modality: rhythm soothes — dancing, drumming, rocking relieve as nothing else; the tarantella itself.
  • Rolling and jerking. Constant changing of position; jerking of single muscles; chorea-like motions in the books.
  • Hyperaesthesia. The whole body over-sensitive; touch, jar, noise all provoke; clothing feels unbearable.
  • Headache relief by rhythm. Violent headaches better for rubbing, rocking and music — movement as medicine.
  • Cardiac flutter. Palpitations with the excitement; anguish in the precordium.
  • Worse afternoon and evening. The state climbs toward evening, easing after the nightly energy is spent.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Nervous system (motor restlessness), heart, emotional life, senses.

Where the classical picture comes from

The wolf spider's bite caused agitation and restlessness in the old reports; the tarantella — the dance that 'cured' it — gave the remedy its famous music-and-motion keynote. The proving completed a portrait of rhythmic, destructive, hyper-sensitive energy.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Spider venoms are a serious modern research field: tarantula toxins that modulate ion channels are studied in pain and cardiac pharmacology, and widow-spider antivenom is real, life-saving medicine. The dance-cure legend survives in the tarantella itself — folk therapy that contained a grain of truth: rhythmic movement genuinely calms an agitated nervous system.
