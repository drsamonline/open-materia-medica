Thiosinaminum

Made from allyl thiourea — the synthetic mustard-oil compound of the old scar-tissue remedy, and the classical remedy of the stricture and the hardened scar.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of tissue hardened beyond its time: scar tissue that tightens and shortens, strictures of the passages, adhesions after inflammation, the books' great softener of the hardened — from a synthetic mustard-oil chemistry made in the laboratory. The scar-softener: that is Thiosinaminum.

The substance, in plain words

Thiosinaminum is allyl thiourea — a synthetic mustard-oil compound made in the laboratory, chosen for its relation to the mustard family and used in the old school specifically to soften scar tissue. Its chemistry is genuinely active on tissue; the proving gave the remedy the stricture-and-scar portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety about health. Worry over the strictures, the scars, the tightening — worse at night.
  • Irritability. Cross from the constant discomfort.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic, hardening states.

The body

  • Scar tissue that tightens. The keynote: scars and fibrous tissue that contract, tighten and shorten — the books' great softener.
  • Strictures of the passages. Strictures of urethra, oesophagus and rectum of the old surgical chapter.
  • Adhesions after inflammation. The adhesions of the abdomen and the passages after old inflammation.
  • Hardened glands and tissue. The indurated tissue of the same chapter — the softener's wider territory.
  • Worse cold damp and night. Wet weather and night deepen the tightening.
  • Better for warmth and time. Warmth and the slow softening of time ease the hardened.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Where the classical picture comes from

The synthetic mustard-compound's proving gave the scar-and-stricture portrait; Thiosinaminum became the classical remedy of the hardened — the old school's specific for what tightens.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Thiosinaminum's real career is the history of the scar-treatment era: used for decades for strictures and adhesions before modern surgery — dilation, excision, endoscopic release — made the chemistry redundant. The strictures it softened are now surgical territory; the compound's career is a chapter in the history of conservative treatment.
