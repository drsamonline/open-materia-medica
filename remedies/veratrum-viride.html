Veratrum viride

Made from American hellebore — the swamp lily whose alkaloids genuinely lower blood pressure and pulse, once an official fever drug, and the classical remedy of the full hard pulse.

The whole picture, in one breath

Think of a fever with a pounding engine: intense heat with a full, hard, bounding pulse, the whole head and face congested and bursting, the heart pounding visibly at the chest wall, worse for the least movement or noise — the portrait of the overloaded circulation. The full-hard-pulse fever: that is Veratrum viride.

The substance, in plain words

American hellebore is the swamp lily of the eastern states, its veratrum alkaloids genuinely depressing heart rate and blood pressure — so reliably that it was an official antipyretic and cardiac drug of the nineteenth century, used in fevers and eclampsia until safer agents came. The proving gave the remedy its congestive portrait.

The traditional picture Classical sources

The inner state

  • Anxiety with the pounding. Fear, restlessness, worse at night with the congestive throbbing.
  • Irritability. Cross, worse from noise and movement, which renew the pounding.
  • Despondency. Gloomy in the chronic congested states.

The body

  • Full hard bounding pulse. The keynote: pulse full, hard, bounding — the engine of the circulation running over-full.
  • Congested bursting head. Intense heat and fullness of the head and face, worse stooping and moving.
  • Visible pounding of the heart. The books describe the heart's impulse visible at the chest wall — the hellebore pharmacology in the portrait.
  • Worse motion and noise. The least movement or sound renews the congestion; the sufferer lies perfectly still.
  • Fever of the overloaded type. The high, congestive, full-pulse fevers the drug itself once treated.
  • Better for stillness and quiet. Perfect stillness, darkness and quiet let the overloaded engine idle.

Where it acts (organ affinities)

Heart and circulation, head and congestion, fever mechanism, nervous system.

Where the classical picture comes from

The official hellebore drug's genuine pharmacology — slowing pulse, lowering pressure — wrote the proving: the full hard pulse, the visible pounding, the congestive fever. Veratrum viride was both drug and remedy in the same era, the one measuring the other.

The substance in real medicine Pharmacology

What this section is: the pharmacology and toxicology of the source substance. It is not clinical evidence that a homeopathic preparation of it treats any condition. What controlled trials do and do not show is set out on the Evidence page.

Veratrum alkaloids were genuine, working pharmacology: American hellebore was an official antipyretic and was used in eclampsia and heart conditions well into the twentieth century in some traditions. Its toxicity ended its career — the line between dose and poison too fine — but the chemistry is real pharmacology, documented: the hellebore that slowed the pulse and the books that recorded it.
